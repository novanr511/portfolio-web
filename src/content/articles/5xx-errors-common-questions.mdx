## Common questions

**What does a 5xx mean?** The server failed to fulfil a valid request, which RFC 9110 defines for the 5xx class, as the status article explains for diagnosis. It is not a client error, as the 4xx class covers those, as the 404 article treats a missing resource as a different case.

**Is one 5xx a problem?** A single failure is usually noise; a sustained rate is a signal, which the SRE alerting workbook treats as alerting over a window, as the error-budget article explains, as the uptime article treats crawling reliability over time.

**What causes them?** Application faults, overloaded dependencies, timeouts, and bad deploys, which the incident article treats as a response problem, as the incident-response-at-scale article advises for containment, as the tracing article recommends for observing a system over time.

**How do I find the cause?** From logs and traces, which the server-logs article describes for what was requested, as the tracing method advises for observing a system over time, as the SRE workbook advises for tying the signal to an objective.

Three consequences follow. The rate should be read against an objective, as the SRE workbook requires, as the SLO article advises. Recent changes should be checked, as the monitoring article treats a deploy as a review trigger, as the rollback article advises. And the result should be recorded, as the decision-memo article requires.
