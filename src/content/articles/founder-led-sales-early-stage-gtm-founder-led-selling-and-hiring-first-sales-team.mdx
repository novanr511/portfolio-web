No one sells your vision better than you. Until you can't.

**Direct answer:** Founder-led sales is the phase where founders personally conduct sales — discovery, demo, negotiation, close — to validate product-market fit, refine messaging, and generate early revenue. The transition to a sales team happens when: PMF is proven, repeatable motion exists, and founder time becomes the bottleneck. The transition requires deliberate hiring, process codification, and cultural integration.

## Why founder-led sales first

- **Signal fidelity:** founders hear raw feedback, not filtered through reps.
- **Speed:** no hiring/onboarding lag. Immediate iteration.
- **Credibility:** prospects talk to decision-maker, not junior rep.
- **Product insight:** direct exposure shapes roadmap.
- **Cost efficiency:** no CAC for sales headcount pre-PMF.

## Founder sales mechanics

**Discovery calls:** 20–30 min. Questions: current workflow, pain, current solutions, decision criteria, timeline, budget authority. Listen 70%, talk 30%.

**Demo:** tailored to discovered pain. Not feature tour. "Here's how we solve X." Leave time for objections.

**Objection handling:** listen → clarify → reframe → evidence (case study, data, demo) → confirm.

**Closing:** mutual action plan (MAP) with milestones, decision criteria, timeline, stakeholders. Ask for the business: "Ready to move forward?"

**Follow-up:** same-day summary email, next steps, calendar invite.

## When to hire the first salesperson

**Signals you're ready:**
- **Repeatable motion:** 10+ closed deals, similar cycle, similar objections.
- **Founder bottleneck:** >50% time on sales, product/strategy neglected.
- **Predictable pipeline:** inbound > outbound, consistent conversion rates.
- **Revenue threshold:** $1–3M ARR (varies by ACV).
- **Process documented:** pitch deck, demo script, objection handlers, proposal template.

**First hire profile:** "Founder's right hand" — high agency, coachable, low ego, writes own emails, comfortable with ambiguity. Often: former founder, top 1% AE, or high-potential junior with grit.

**Avoid:** "coin-operated" reps who need perfect leads, scripts, collateral.

## Building the first sales team

**Hiring sequence:**
1. **Founder + 1 AE** (founder closes, AE learns).
2. **Founder + 2–3 AEs** (founder steps back from closing).
3. **Sales Manager / Head of Sales** (when 4+ AEs, founder fully out of closing).
4. **SDR/BDR** (when inbound > AE capacity, or outbound needed).
5. **Sales Enablement / Ops** (when 8+ reps).

**Compensation for early reps:** lower base (60–70% OTE), higher variable, equity (0.1–0.5%). OTE = 1.5–2x base.

**Ramp:** 30 days shadowing → 30 days co-selling → 30 days solo with check-ins.

## Codifying the playbook

Before hiring, document:
- **ICP & personas:** firmographics, titles, pain, trigger events.
- **Pitch deck:** 10 slides, problem → solution → proof → ask.
- **Demo script:** modular, persona-based, objection-triggered branches.
- **Objection library:** top 10 objections, best responses.
- **Pricing / proposal template:** packages, discount authority, terms.
- **CRM stages:** definitions, exit criteria, required fields.
- **Forecast methodology:** stage-weighted, rep commit, hygiene standards.

## Cultural integration

- **Founder stays close:** weekly pipeline review, monthly ride-alongs, quarterly field days.
- **Revenue culture:** celebrate wins, dissect losses (blameless), share customer stories.
- **Product feedback loop:** weekly "voice of customer" to product, feature requests tagged by ARR impact.

## Common mistakes

- **Hiring too early:** before PMF, before repeatable motion.
- **Hiring "stars" from big co:** they need infrastructure you don't have.
- **Over-hiring SDRs before AEs:** SDRs need AE capacity to hand off to.
- **Under-compensating:** equity-heavy, cash-light repels talent.
- **Founder abdicates:** stays in weeds, doesn't build machine.
