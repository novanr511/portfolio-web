AI search has produced a growing list of proposed tactics: special schema, AI-readable files, prompt-targeted pages, and promises of citations. Website owners need to separate documented controls from speculation—and distinguish Google's own Search features from other answer engines.

**Direct answer:** For Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google says the same foundational SEO best practices apply and that there are no additional technical requirements or special optimizations for inclusion. A page must meet Google's Search technical requirements and be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet, but eligibility does not guarantee crawling, indexing, or serving. Focus on accessible, useful, people-first content; crawlable internal links; accurate structured data when relevant; and correct preview controls. Measure the Search Console data Google actually reports, and do not treat a citation, ranking, or traffic outcome as guaranteed.

## 1. First define what “AI search” means

AI Overviews and AI Mode are features within Google Search. Other answer engines have their own crawlers, policies, retrieval systems, and reporting. Google's documentation describes Google's products; it does not establish how every AI search service works.

Google says AI Overviews can summarize a topic and provide links for further exploration, while AI Mode supports more exploratory or complex queries and links to supporting websites. Its documentation also says these features may use a “query fan-out” technique—related searches across subtopics and sources—and that AI Overviews do not appear for every query. These are descriptions of Google's systems, not a public recipe for choosing citations or a guarantee that a particular page will appear.[^ai-features]

## 2. What Google documents—and what it does not

Google's guidance makes three distinctions important for technical SEO:

1. **Eligibility is not a promise.** A page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet to be eligible as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Even meeting requirements and best practices does not guarantee that Google will crawl, index, or serve the page.[^ai-features][^technical]
2. **There is no special AI markup requirement.** Google says no new machine-readable files, AI text files, or special Schema.org structured data are required to appear in these features. Structured data should still accurately represent visible page content, but it is not a citation switch.[^ai-features]
3. **Search controls remain relevant.** Google identifies robots.txt access for Googlebot and preview controls such as `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, and `max-snippet` as ways to manage crawling or the information shown from pages in Search. Those controls affect what Google can access or use; they do not guarantee a desired placement.[^ai-features][^robots]

This is why “GEO” claims should be tested like any other claim: ask which engine, which documented mechanism, and what observable result supports the recommendation. A vendor's suggestion is not an official requirement merely because it uses the term “AI search.”

## 3. A practical technical checklist

Use this checklist for Google Search's AI features without treating it as a ranking formula:

### Make important pages accessible and understandable

* Ensure Googlebot is not blocked from pages intended for Search, and that pages return a working response with indexable content. Google's technical requirements are minimum eligibility conditions, not an indexing guarantee.[^technical]
* Link important pages through crawlable internal links and maintain useful site navigation. Google's AI-features guidance specifically recommends making content findable through internal links.[^ai-features]
* Make important information available as text. Supplement it with images or video when they genuinely help the page, rather than hiding essential meaning only in media or interaction.[^ai-features]
* Check rendered content and required assets when JavaScript is involved. A live rendering test can help diagnose access or rendering problems; it cannot promise indexing.

### Keep page signals accurate

* Use a clear page title and useful headings that describe the content rather than attempting to mimic an AI response format.
* Add structured data only when it describes content that is actually visible and relevant to the page. Google's feature guidance says special schema is not required for AI feature inclusion.[^ai-features]
* Check canonicals, status codes, robots directives, and sitemap consistency as part of standard technical maintenance. A `200` response alone does not make a page indexable or guarantee indexing.[^technical]

### Decide deliberately about previews

Google lists `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, and `max-snippet` as controls for limiting what it shows or uses from a page in Search AI features. The robots meta documentation states that these instructions are read only when the crawler can access the page. Blocking a URL in robots.txt can therefore prevent Google from reading a page-level control on that URL.[^ai-features][^robots]

Before restricting previews, understand the trade-off: a control intended to limit display or use can also affect the search result preview. Test the directive on the intended page type and verify what Googlebot received with URL Inspection. Do not apply a site-wide rule based on an unverified assumption about citations.

Google-Extended is a separate control mentioned for limiting use in some other Google systems; Google describes it in the context of training and grounding outside Search. Do not treat it as a switch for opting out of AI Overviews or AI Mode, which Google's guidance describes as part of Search.[^ai-features]

## 4. How to measure without overclaiming

Google says traffic from sites appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode is included in Search Console's overall Performance report under the **Web** search type. The standard report offers metrics such as clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position, and can be grouped by dimensions including query, page, country, device, search appearance, and date.[^ai-features][^performance]

That supports monitoring overall Search performance, but it does not justify attributing every change in clicks or impressions to an AI feature. Use a careful measurement process:

1. Record the pages, query groups, date range, country, and device being examined.
2. Compare like-for-like periods and note changes in the site, seasonality, and other search features.
3. Use Search Console and analytics together where appropriate, while keeping their distinct definitions and attribution limits in view.
4. Report observed clicks, impressions, and conversions separately from hypotheses about why they changed.

Search Console data is not a guarantee that a particular citation was shown, and a traffic trend alone cannot establish which interface caused it. Google's help documentation also notes that the newest report data can be preliminary and may change.[^performance]

For a broader report-by-report diagnostic workflow, see [Google Search Console for Technical SEO: A Diagnostic Workflow](/articles/google-search-console-for-technical-seo-a-diagnostic-workflow).

## 5. A simple decision test for GEO recommendations

Before implementing advice marketed as “AI SEO,” ask:

| Question | Evidence-based response |
| :--- | :--- |
| Is this an official requirement for the specific engine? | Find and read that engine's primary documentation. For Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google says there are no additional technical requirements. |
| Does the tactic change crawling, indexing eligibility, or preview controls? | Identify the exact mechanism and side effects; test it on a limited set of URLs. |
| Does the recommendation promise citations or traffic? | Treat the promise as unsupported unless it comes with a reproducible method and reliable evidence. Google's documentation does not guarantee inclusion. |
| Can the outcome be measured? | Define the observable metric first; distinguish it from an assumed citation or causal explanation. |
| Is the advice being generalized across answer engines? | Do not assume one platform's crawler rules or reporting apply to another. |

## Summary

For Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, the safest technical approach is ordinary, well-executed Search fundamentals—not a special “AI file” or schema trick. Keep intended pages accessible, useful, internally discoverable, and technically eligible; use preview controls only when their consequences are understood; and describe Search Console results as the aggregate data Google documents. For other answer engines, verify their own primary documentation rather than extrapolating Google's behavior.
