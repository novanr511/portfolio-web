Category design is often described as creating a market, which sounds like a one-time act of naming. The measurable part is quieter: whether buyers use a new frame to evaluate options, whether the frame changes what they compare, and whether the change holds beyond the launch, as the blue-ocean article treats a new value curve as a strategic move, as the category-design article argues for creating a distinct space. A name that nobody adopts is not a category.

**Direct answer:** Category design should be measured by changes in buyer framing, comparison set, and willingness to pay, against a baseline. ## A worked example

The following is hypothetical and illustrative. A company positions a product as a new category rather than as a cheaper alternative in an existing one.
