## A counterargument

A reasonable critic can object that the article's per-channel discipline assumes boundaries the sources do not draw so cleanly. The distribution entry describes a route from producer to customer, and the reverse-logistics entry describes the return flow, but both treat overlapping systems: shared inventory, a store that fulfils online orders, a customer who buys in one channel and returns in another. The article itself notes such sharing, which undercuts its own prescription to model economics per channel.

<PullQuote>
The objection is not that channel costs are unreal; it is that the two references support a structural description, not the decision rule built on them.
</PullQuote>

Under conditions where channels are complementary rather than parallel, a per-channel margin model can misallocate shared cost and reverse the conclusion the framework is meant to deliver.
