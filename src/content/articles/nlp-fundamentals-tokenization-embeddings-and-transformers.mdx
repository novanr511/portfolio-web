Text is unstructured. NLP makes it computable.

**Direct answer:** NLP (Natural Language Processing) converts human language into structured representations that machines can process. The pipeline: tokenization breaks text into units, embeddings map tokens to dense vectors, and transformers model contextual relationships. Modern NLP is dominated by transformer architectures (BERT, GPT, T5) that capture bidirectional context and enable transfer learning.

## Tokenization: from text to tokens

**Word-level:** split on whitespace and punctuation. Large vocabulary, OOV (out-of-vocabulary) issues.

**Subword:** Byte Pair Encoding (BPE), WordPiece, Unigram. Splits rare words into known subwords. Used by BERT (WordPiece), GPT (BPE), T5 (SentencePiece). Balances vocabulary size and OOV rate.

**Character-level:** no OOV, but longer sequences, harder to learn long-range dependencies.

**SentencePiece / BPE:** unsupervised segmentation, language-agnostic. Standard for multilingual models.

## Embeddings: from tokens to vectors

**Static embeddings:** Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText. Fixed vector per token. FastText handles OOV via subword n-grams. Context-independent (same vector for "bank" in "river bank" and "bank account").

**Contextual embeddings:** ELMo, BERT, RoBERTa, GPT. Vector depends on surrounding context. "Bank" has different vectors in different sentences. Produced by transformer encoders/decoders.

**Sentence embeddings:** Sentence-BERT, Universal Sentence Encoder. Fixed-size vector for entire sentence/paragraph. Used for semantic similarity, clustering, retrieval.

## Transformer architecture

**Attention mechanism:** scaled dot-product attention computes weighted sum of values based on query-key compatibility. Multi-head attention runs multiple attention heads in parallel.

**Encoder-decoder:** encoder processes input sequence; decoder generates output autoregressively (translation, summarization).

**Encoder-only (BERT-style):** bidirectional context. Pretrained with masked language modeling (MLM) and next sentence prediction. Fine-tuned for classification, NER, QA.

**Decoder-only (GPT-style):** causal/unidirectional attention. Pretrained with next-token prediction. Generates text. Foundation for LLMs.

**Encoder-decoder (T5, BART):** sequence-to-sequence. Pretrained with denoising objectives. Used for translation, summarization, question answering.

## Practical considerations

- **Pretraining vs fine-tuning:** massive corpora pretraining, then task-specific fine-tuning on labeled data.
- **Tokenization alignment:** ensure tokenizer matches pretrained model.
- **Sequence length limits:** BERT 512, GPT-3 2048, newer models 32k–128k+. Use sliding windows or hierarchical encoding for long docs.
- **Efficiency:** distillation, quantization, LoRA, adapters, quantization-aware training for deployment.

## Further Reading

- Ashish Vaswani et al., 2017. [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) — The transformer architecture that the NLP and LLM sections build on.
- Jacob Devlin et al., 2019. [BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805) — Bidirectional encoder pretraining and the masked-language-model objective.
- Rico Sennrich, Barry Haddow, and Alexandra Birch, 2015. [Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.07909) — The subword vocabulary approach that fixed out-of-vocabulary problems.
- Taku Kudo and John Richardson, 2018. [SentencePiece: A simple and language independent subword tokenizer and detokenizer for Neural Text Processing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06226) — The tokenizer family used by T5, Llama, and most modern open models.
- Nils Reimers and Iryna Gurevych, 2019. [Sentence-BERT: Sentence Embeddings using Siamese BERT-Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084) — The bi-encoder recipe behind modern semantic search and RAG retrieval.
