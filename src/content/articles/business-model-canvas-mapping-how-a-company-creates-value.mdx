A business model is not a business plan. It is a map of how value flows.

**Direct answer:** The Business Model Canvas is a strategic tool that describes how a company creates, delivers, and captures value through nine building blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, and cost structure. It is used to visualize, test, and refine a business model before writing a full business plan. The canvas is a thinking tool, not a financial document.

## The nine building blocks

**1. Customer Segments.** Who are the customers? Different segments have different needs and willingness to pay.

**2. Value Propositions.** What value is delivered to each segment? This solves a problem or meets a need.

**3. Channels.** How is the value proposition communicated and delivered? Sales, distribution, marketing channels.

**4. Customer Relationships.** How does the company get, keep, and grow customers? Personal assistance, self-service, communities.

**5. Revenue Streams.** How does the company make money? Sales, subscriptions, licensing, advertising, fees.

**6. Key Resources.** What assets are required? People, technology, intellectual property, physical assets, financial resources.

**7. Key Activities.** What must the company do well? Production, problem-solving, platform management.

**8. Key Partnerships.** Who helps the business operate? Suppliers, distributors, alliances.

**9. Cost Structure.** What are the major costs? Fixed, variable, economies of scale.

## How to use the canvas

The canvas is designed to be filled quickly, on a wall or whiteboard, with sticky notes. It is meant to be revised as assumptions are tested:

1. **Fill in what you know.** Start with customer segments and value propositions—the core of the model.
2. **Identify assumptions.** Which blocks are guesses? Which need testing?
3. **Test the riskiest assumptions first.** If the value proposition is wrong, nothing else matters.
4. **Revise.** Update the canvas as evidence accumulates.

## Canvas versus business plan

A business plan is a detailed document for investors and lenders. It includes financial projections, market analysis, and operational plans. It takes weeks to write and is often outdated before it is finished.

The canvas is a one-page sketch for exploration. It takes hours to create and is meant to change. Use the canvas to figure out the business; use the plan to communicate it to outsiders.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the canvas as complete.** It is a hypothesis, not a fact.
- **Ignoring cost structure.** Many founders focus on revenue and forget costs.
- **Not defining segments.** "Everyone" is not a customer segment.
- **Confusing activity with value.** Key activities must create value for customers, not just keep the company busy.

## When to use it

Use the canvas when:

- Starting a new business.
- Launching a new product or service.
- Evaluating a pivot.
- Exploring a new market segment.
- Teaching business model thinking.
