Real estate is the world's largest asset class. Understanding its mechanics unlocks diversification and inflation protection.

**Direct answer:** Real estate markets encompass residential, commercial, industrial, and land segments. Investment vehicles include direct ownership, REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts), private equity funds, and crowdfunding platforms. Key drivers: interest rates, demographic trends, zoning, and economic cycles. Valuation methods: cap rate, DCF, comparable sales, and replacement cost.

## Real estate segments

**Residential:** single-family, multifamily, condos. Drivers: household formation, affordability, migration.

**Commercial (CRE):** office, retail, industrial, multifamily (5+ units). Leases: gross, net, triple-net (NNN). Value driven by NOI and cap rate.

**Industrial / Logistics:** warehouses, distribution centers, cold storage. Beneficiary of e-commerce growth. Low vacancy, rising rents.

**Land:** development, speculation, agriculture. Highest risk/return, illiquid, entitlement risk.

## REITs: liquid real estate exposure

REITs are companies that own income-producing real estate. Requirements (US): ≥75% assets in real estate, ≥75% income from rents/mortgages, ≥90% taxable income distributed as dividends.

**Types:**
- **Equity REITs:** own properties, collect rent. Majority of market.
- **Mortgage REITs (mREITs):** hold mortgages/MBS, earn interest spread. Higher leverage, interest rate sensitive.
- **Hybrid REITs:** both.

**Metrics:** FFO (Funds From Operations), AFFO (Adjusted FFO), NAV (Net Asset Value), dividend yield, payout ratio, debt/EBITDA.

## Property cycles

Real estate moves in cycles (typically 7-10 years):

1. **Recovery:** low occupancy, no construction, buying opportunities.
2. **Expansion:** rising occupancy, rent growth, new construction starts.
3. **Hyper-supply:** oversupply, vacancy rises, rent growth slows.
4. **Recession:** high vacancy, negative rent growth, distress.

Timing varies by sector and geography. Industrial leads; office lags.

## Valuation methods

- **Cap rate:** NOI / Property Value. Inverse of yield. Compression = price appreciation.
- **DCF:** project cash flows, discount at required return. Captures lease rollovers, capex.
- **Comparable sales:** recent transactions, adjusted for differences.
- **Replacement cost:** land + construction cost - depreciation. Floor for value.

## Portfolio integration

Real estate provides: income yield, inflation hedge (lease escalators), diversification (low correlation with stocks/bonds), tax advantages (depreciation, 1031 exchange).

**Allocation:** 5-15% typical institutional allocation. REITs for liquidity; direct/private for control and alpha.
