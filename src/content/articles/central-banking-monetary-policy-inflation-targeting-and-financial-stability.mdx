Central banks are the lenders of last resort and guardians of monetary stability.

**Direct answer:** Central banks implement monetary policy to achieve price stability, full employment, and financial stability. Tools: policy rates, open market operations, reserve requirements, forward guidance, quantitative easing/tightening. Inflation targeting anchors expectations. Balance sheet operations (QE/QT) affect term premia and credit conditions. Independence and credibility are prerequisites for policy effectiveness.

## Mandates

**Dual mandate (Fed):** maximum employment + stable prices (2% inflation).

**Single mandate (ECB, BoE, BoJ):** price stability primary; support growth/employment secondary.

**Financial stability:** macroprudential regulation, lender of last resort, systemic risk monitoring.

## Policy tools

**Policy rate:** federal funds rate (US), deposit facility rate (ECB), base rate (BoE). Anchors short-term rates.

**Open market operations (OMO):** repos/reverse repos, outright purchases/sales. Manage liquidity.

**Standing facilities:** marginal lending (ceiling), deposit (floor). Corridor system.

**Reserve requirements:** fraction of deposits held at central bank. Less used in modern frameworks.

**Forward guidance:** communication about future policy path. Manages expectations.

**Quantitative Easing (QE):** large-scale asset purchases (govt bonds, MBS, corporates). Lowers long-term yields, portfolio rebalancing, signaling.

**Quantitative Tightening (QT):** balance sheet runoff or active sales. Drains liquidity.

## Inflation targeting

**Target:** typically 2% (symmetrical or average). Anchors long-term expectations.

**Transmission mechanism:** policy rate → market rates → credit conditions → asset prices → exchange rates → demand → output gap → inflation.

**Challenges:** supply shocks (oil, supply chains), anchored vs unanchored expectations, zero lower bound (ZLB), fiscal dominance.

**Average Inflation Targeting (AIT):** allow overshoot after undershoot (Fed 2020).

## Balance sheet policies

**QE channels:** portfolio rebalancing, signaling, liquidity, credit easing.

**QT:** passive runoff (caps) or active sales. Reduces reserves, raises term premium.

**Risks:** central bank losses, market functioning, fiscal interaction.

## Financial stability toolkit

**Macroprudential tools:** CCyB (countercyclical capital buffer), sectoral capital requirements, LTV/DTI limits, systemic risk buffer.

**Lender of last resort:** Bagehot's rule: lend freely, at penalty rate, on good collateral.

**Resolution regimes:** bail-in, resolution funds, TLAC/MREL.

## Independence and accountability

**Instrument independence:** central bank sets tools. **Goal independence:** sets targets (rare).

**Accountability:** testimony, reports, minutes, transparency.

**Credibility:** built through consistent action. Lowers inflation expectations, reduces sacrifice ratio.
