Better is not enough. Different is what wins attention.

**Direct answer:** Brand positioning is the act of occupying a distinct, meaningful place in the customer's mind relative to competitors. Being "better" is hard to prove and easy to copy. Being different—serving a specific customer, solving a specific problem, in a specific way—creates a position that competitors cannot easily attack. Positioning is not a slogan; it is a strategic choice about who you are for and who you are not for.

## What positioning is

Positioning answers three questions:

- **Who is the product for?** (target customer)
- **What does it do?** (category and benefit)
- **Why is it different?** (reason to believe)

A product without positioning is just a tool. A product with positioning is a choice.

## Why "better" fails

Claims of being better—better quality, better service, better features—are:

- **Hard to verify.** Customers cannot easily compare.
- **Easy to copy.** Competitors can match or exceed features.
- **Subjective.** "Better" depends on the customer's situation.

A product that is better for everyone is often best for no one. It tries to please all segments and ends up resonating with none.

## Why differentiation works

Differentiation creates a position competitors cannot occupy without changing their own identity:

- **A project manager for creative agencies** is different from "a project manager for everyone."
- **A bank for freelancers** is different from "a bank for everyone."
- **A CRM for e-commerce brands** is different from "a CRM for everyone."

The narrower the focus, the stronger the position. Customers choose the product that feels made for them, not the product that claims to be best for everyone.

## How to position

1. **Pick a segment.** Who has the most painful problem you solve?
2. **Name the category.** What market do you belong to, and how are you different within it?
3. **Identify the unique value.** What do you offer that the segment cares about and competitors do not?
4. **State who you are not for.** Explicit exclusion sharpens inclusion.
5. **Test in the market.** Does the target segment recognize itself in your positioning?

## Positioning is not marketing copy

Positioning is a strategic decision that informs product, pricing, distribution, and marketing. It is not a tagline written by an agency. If the product does not deliver on the position, no amount of messaging will save it.

## When to reposition

Markets change. Competitors move. Customer needs evolve. A position that worked two years ago may be crowded today. Repositioning is not failure; it is adaptation. The risk is not repositioning; it is staying in a position that no one cares about.
