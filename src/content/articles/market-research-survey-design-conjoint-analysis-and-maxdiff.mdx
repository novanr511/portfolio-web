Good decisions need good data. Market research provides the evidence.

**Direct answer:** Market research systematically gathers and analyzes data about customers, competitors, and markets to reduce uncertainty in business decisions. Core quantitative methods: survey design (questionnaire construction, sampling, bias mitigation), conjoint analysis (attribute importance, willingness-to-pay), MaxDiff (preference ranking), and qualitative methods (interviews, focus groups, ethnography). The goal: actionable insights, not just data.

## Survey design fundamentals

**Question types:**
- **Dichotomous:** yes/no.
- **Multiple choice:** single/multiple select.
- **Likert scale:** 5/7-point agree-disagree.
- **Semantic differential:** bipolar adjectives.
- **Ranking:** order preferences.
- **Open-ended:** verbatims for qualitative depth.

**Design principles:**
- **One concept per question.** No double-barreled ("fast and reliable").
- **Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive (MECE)** response options.
- **Neutral wording:** avoid leading ("How much did you love...").
- **Randomize order:** mitigate order bias.
- **Screeners:** qualify respondents upfront.

**Sampling:**
- **Probability:** random, stratified, cluster. Generalizable.
- **Non-probability:** quota, convenience, panel. Faster, cheaper, bias risk.
- **Sample size:** power analysis (margin of error, confidence level, population variance).

## Conjoint analysis

Conjoint reveals how people value attributes by forcing trade-offs.

**Types:**
- **Choice-based conjoint (CBC):** choose among product profiles. Most realistic.
- **Adaptive CBC (ACBC):** adaptive, efficient for many attributes.
- **Choice-based menu:** build-your-own.

**Outputs:**
- **Part-worth utilities:** utility per level per attribute.
- **Attribute importance:** % contribution to choice.
- **Willingness-to-pay (WTP):** dollar value per attribute level.
- **Market simulation:** share prediction for new configurations.

**Design:** fractional factorial design (orthogonal, level balance, minimal overlap). Sample size: 200–500+ per segment.

## MaxDiff (Maximum Difference Scaling)

MaxDiff (Best-Worst Scaling) forces choices: "Best" and "Worst" from a set.

**Why not rating scales?** ratings suffer from scale bias (straight-lining, central tendency). MaxDiff forces discrimination.

**Design:** items presented in subsets (3–5 per screen). Each item appears equally often. Balanced incomplete block design (BIBD).

**Outputs:** preference shares (probability each item is "best"), utility scores. More discriminating than ratings.

**Use cases:** feature prioritization, message testing, brand attribute importance, claim substantiation.

## Qualitative methods

**In-depth interviews (IDIs):** 30–60 min, semi-structured guide. Probe "why."

**Focus groups:** 6–8 participants, moderated discussion. Group dynamics reveal norms.

**Ethnography / contextual inquiry:** observe users in context. Uncovers unarticulated needs.

**Diary studies:** longitudinal self-reporting. Captures real-time behavior.

**Analysis:** thematic coding (open, axial, selective), affinity mapping, journey mapping.

## From insights to decisions

**Insight framework:** Observation → Interpretation → Implication → Recommendation.

**Actionability test:** "If this is true, what would we do differently?" If no action → not actionable.

**Stakeholder communication:** executive summary (1 page), key findings (3–5), evidence appendix, decision framework.

## Common pitfalls

- **Confirmation bias:** designing to confirm hypothesis.
- **Leading questions:** "Don't you think..."
- **Sample bias:** convenience sample ≠ target market.
- **Over-surveying:** fatigue, low response rates, poor quality.
- **Ignoring non-responders:** non-response bias.
- **Analysis paralysis:** perfect data doesn't exist. Decide with 70% confidence.
