SEO and paid advertising both bring customers. They do it differently.

**Direct answer:** SEO (search engine optimization) and paid advertising are two customer acquisition channels with different economics. SEO builds long-term, compounding traffic but takes time and consistent effort. Paid advertising delivers immediate traffic but stops when spending stops. The right choice depends on cash flow, timeline, margins, and risk tolerance. Most successful businesses use both, but understand the economics of each before allocating budget.

## How SEO works

SEO improves a site's visibility in organic search results. It involves technical health (crawlability, speed, mobile), content quality, and authority (links, reputation). Results take months to materialize but can compound: a well-ranked page keeps delivering traffic without ongoing payment per click.

SEO economics:
- **Upfront cost:** content creation, technical fixes, link building.
- **Ongoing cost:** maintenance, content updates, monitoring.
- **Marginal cost per visitor:** near zero once ranked.
- **Timeline:** 3–12 months for meaningful results.
- **Risk:** algorithm changes, competitor moves, indexing issues.

## How paid advertising works

Paid advertising (search ads, social ads, display ads) buys placement. You pay per click, per impression, or per acquisition. Traffic starts immediately when campaigns launch and stops when budget ends.

Paid advertising economics:
- **Upfront cost:** ad creative, campaign setup, landing pages.
- **Ongoing cost:** media spend, management, optimization.
- **Marginal cost per visitor:** the cost per click (CPC) or cost per acquisition (CPA).
- **Timeline:** immediate.
- **Risk:** rising CPCs, ad fatigue, platform policy changes, budget exhaustion.

## Comparing the two

| Factor | SEO | Paid Advertising |
|---|---|---|
| Speed to traffic | Months | Days |
| Cost structure | Upfront + ongoing | Per click/impression |
| Traffic after stopping | Continues (decays slowly) | Stops immediately |
| Targeting | Broad (search intent) | Precise (demographics, behavior) |
| Control | Limited (algorithm decides) | High (budget, audience, creative) |
| Risk | Algorithm changes | Cost inflation, budget limits |

## When to choose SEO

SEO is right when:

- You have time to wait for results (6–12 months).
- You can invest in content and technical quality.
- You want sustainable, compounding traffic.
- Your customers search for solutions like yours.

SEO is wrong when you need revenue this month or your customers do not use search.

## When to choose paid advertising

Paid advertising is right when:

- You need immediate traffic or revenue.
- You have a clear CPA target and unit economics that support it.
- You are testing a new offer or market.
- Your customers are reachable through targeting options.

Paid advertising is wrong when margins are too thin to support CPA, or when you cannot sustain ongoing spend.

## The hybrid approach

Most successful businesses use both:

- Paid for immediate traffic, testing, and specific campaigns.
- SEO for long-term, compounding visibility.
- Data from paid campaigns informs SEO content priorities (which keywords convert).
- SEO reduces paid dependency over time.

The allocation shifts as the business matures: early-stage relies more on paid for speed; mature businesses shift toward SEO for margin.

## Measuring acquisition economics

Compare channels on the same basis:

- **CAC by channel:** total spend divided by customers acquired.
- **LTV by channel:** do SEO customers stay longer than paid customers?
- **Payback period:** how fast does each channel recover its cost?
- **Assisted vs last-click:** SEO often assists conversions that paid ads get credit for.

Attribution is imperfect, but comparing channels on consistent metrics prevents overinvesting in one.
