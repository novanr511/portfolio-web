The product is the marketing. The product is the sales.

**Direct answer:** Product-Led Growth (PLG) is a go-to-market strategy where the product itself drives acquisition, activation, conversion, and expansion. Users experience value before paying. Key loops: acquisition (viral, SEO, referral), activation (Aha! moment, TTV), retention (habit formation), expansion (usage-based, seats, features). Metrics: PQL, activation rate, NRR, viral coefficient.

## PLG vs sales-led

| Dimension | Sales-Led | Product-Led |
|---|---|---|
| First value | After contract | Before contract (freemium/trial) |
| Buyer | Decision-maker | End-user |
| CAC | High (sales team) | Low (self-serve) |
| Sales cycle | Months | Minutes to days |
| Expansion | Account management | In-product upsell |
| Metrics | MQL, SQL, ARR | PQL, activation, NRR |

## The PLG flywheel

1. **Acquisition:** viral loops (invite, share, embed), SEO/content, referral programs, product-marketplaces.
2. **Activation:** user reaches "Aha! moment" quickly. Time-to-Value (TTV) < 5 min ideal. Onboarding, checklists, tooltips, templates.
3. **Retention:** habit loops, core action frequency, feature adoption. Cohort retention curves.
4. **Expansion:** usage-based pricing, seat expansion, feature upsell, team invitations. Net Revenue Retention (NRR) > 100%.

## Key metrics

- **PQL (Product Qualified Lead):** user who reached activation threshold. Higher conversion than MQL.
- **Activation rate:** % signups reaching Aha! moment.
- **Time-to-Value (TTV):** time from signup to first value realization.
- **Free-to-paid conversion:** % free users converting.
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention):** (Starting MRR + Expansion - Churn - Contraction) / Starting MRR. >100% = growth engine.
- **Viral coefficient (K):** invites sent × conversion rate. K > 1 = viral growth.
- **Feature adoption depth:** % users adopting core features.

## Organizational shifts

- **Product owns revenue:** growth team in product, not marketing.
- **Engineering owns growth:** growth engineers, experimentation platform.
- **Data culture:** self-serve analytics, feature flags, experimentation (A/B testing).
- **Pricing:** usage-based, per-seat, freemium tiers. Transparent, self-serve.

## Common PLG patterns

- **Freemium:** free tier forever, paid for limits/features. Dropbox, Notion, Figma.
- **Free trial:** time-limited full access. Slack, Zoom.
- **Reverse trial:** start on paid trial, downgrade to free if not converted.
- **Open source:** community edition + enterprise. GitLab, Elastic.

## When PLG works (and doesn't)

**Works:** low complexity, high user autonomy, viral potential, bottom-up adoption (dev tools, collab, design).

**Doesn't work:** high complexity, enterprise sales required, regulatory, top-down buying (ERP, security).
