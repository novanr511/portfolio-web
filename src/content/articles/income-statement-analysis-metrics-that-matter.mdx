## The metrics that matter

**Revenue growth and mix.** Growth is a starting question, not an answer, which the valuation article treats as conditional, as the comparables article requires consistent definitions, as the market-definition article advises on defining the market.

**Gross margin.** Revenue minus cost of goods sold, which the EBITDA article explains for what sits above and below the line, as the pricing article treats margin as a constraint, as the unit-economics article requires for contribution.

**Operating margin.** Gross profit minus operating expenses, which the EBITDA article treats as a place where non-cash charges appear, as the profitability article explains for capital intensity, as the cash-flow article requires for reconciliation.

**Below-the-line items.** Interest, tax, and one-offs can dominate net income, which the debt article treats as an obligation, as the tax article treats as jurisdiction-specific, as the earnings-quality article warns about estimates.
