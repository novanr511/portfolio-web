Negotiation happens every day.

**Direct answer:** Negotiation is the process of reaching agreement between parties with different interests. It is not confrontation or haggling; it is structured communication to create and divide value. Good negotiators prepare thoroughly, understand the other side's interests, communicate clearly, and separate people from the problem. Negotiation applies to salary, price, scope, deadlines, and almost every business interaction.

## What negotiation is

Negotiation occurs whenever two or more parties need to agree but do not initially agree on terms. It happens in:

- **Salary discussions.** Candidate and employer on compensation and role.
- **Pricing.** Buyer and seller on cost and terms.
- **Scope.** Client and agency on what is delivered and when.
- **Partnerships.** Two companies on responsibilities and revenue sharing.

The goal is not to "win" at the other's expense. It is to find an agreement both sides accept and can uphold.

## Preparation beats tactics

Most negotiation outcomes are decided before the conversation. Preparation includes:

- **Your interests.** What do you need, and why?
- **Your alternatives.** What do you do if no agreement is reached? (BATNA: Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement.)
- **Their interests.** What do they need, and what constraints do they face?
- **Objective criteria.** Market rates, standards, precedents that support a fair outcome.
- **The opening offer.** Anchors the conversation, but should be defensible.

A strong alternative gives you power to walk away. That power makes your position stronger, not because you use it, because you can.

## Interests versus positions

A position is what you demand ("I want $100,000"). An interest is why you need it ("I need to cover relocation costs and market rates for my role"). Positions clash; interests often align.

Understanding interests opens solutions that positions hide. If salary is fixed, perhaps signing bonus, remote days, or learning budget can move.

## Creating value before dividing

Most negotiations are not zero-sum. Both sides can gain:

- A buyer gets better terms; a seller gets a longer commitment.
- An employee gets flexibility; an employer gets retention.
- A vendor gets volume; a customer gets a discount.

Create value first by understanding interests. Divide value second by agreeing on terms.

## Communication during negotiation

- **Listen more than you speak.** Understand before proposing.
- **Ask open questions.** "What matters most to you in this deal?"
- **Separate people from the problem.** Be hard on the problem, soft on the person.
- **Silence is a tool.** Pauses let the other side think and often reveal information.
- **Document agreements.** Write down what was decided to avoid later disputes.

## Common mistakes

- **Accepting the first offer.** It anchors low; there is usually room.
- **Revealing your bottom line too early.** It eliminates your flexibility.
- **Making concessions without asking for anything.** Concessions should be reciprocal.
- **Negotiating against yourself.** Lowering your offer before the other side responds.
- **Treating it as one transaction.** Reputation matters for future negotiations.

## Salary negotiation

Salary negotiation is one of the highest-ROI conversations in a career. A 10% increase compounds over years through raises and future offers. Research market rates, know your value, practice the conversation, and be prepared to discuss total compensation (salary, equity, benefits, flexibility).
