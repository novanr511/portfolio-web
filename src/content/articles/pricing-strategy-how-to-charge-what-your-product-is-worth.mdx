Pricing is one of the most underrated decisions in business.

**Direct answer:** Pricing is a strategic decision that signals value, positions a product in the market, and determines profitability. It is not simply cost plus margin. Effective pricing aligns with customer willingness to pay, reflects the value delivered, and supports the business model. The right price captures value without discouraging adoption.

## What pricing communicates

Price is a signal. A high price suggests quality, exclusivity, or enterprise capability. A low price suggests accessibility, commoditization, or a loss leader. Customers use price to infer quality before they experience the product. A price that is too low can raise suspicion; a price that is too high can exclude the target market.

## Common pricing models

**Cost-plus pricing.** Add a margin to the cost of production. Simple but ignores customer value and competition. Can leave money on the table or price out the market.

**Value-based pricing.** Charge based on the value the customer receives, not the cost to produce. This captures more value when the product solves a painful problem. The challenge is measuring and communicating that value.

**Competition-based pricing.** Set prices relative to competitors. Useful in crowded markets but risks racing to the bottom and ignoring your own cost structure.

**Subscription pricing.** Recurring revenue for ongoing access. Aligns with continuous value delivery but requires strong retention.

**Usage-based pricing.** Charge by volume: API calls, seats, storage, transactions. Scales with customer value but makes revenue less predictable.

**Tiered pricing.** Different feature sets at different price points. Captures more customer segments but adds complexity.

## The pricing process

1. **Understand costs.** Know the floor: what does it cost to produce, deliver, and support one unit?
2. **Understand value.** What problem does the product solve, and what is that worth to the customer?
3. **Understand competition.** What are alternatives, and what do they charge?
4. **Choose a model.** Match the pricing model to how value is delivered and consumed.
5. **Test.** Prices are hypotheses. Test with real customers, not surveys.
6. **Iterate.** Prices change as costs, competition, and value evolve.

## Common pricing mistakes

- **Underpricing to win customers.** Low prices attract price-sensitive customers who churn easily and erode margins.
- **Overpricing based on internal costs.** Customers do not care about your costs; they care about value.
- **One price for all segments.** Different customers have different willingness to pay. Tiers and plans capture more value.
- **Never changing prices.** Markets change. Prices should too, with clear communication to existing customers.

## Price and positioning are inseparable

You cannot price independently of positioning. A luxury product priced like a commodity confuses customers. A commodity product priced like a luxury loses sales. Price, brand, product, and channel must tell a consistent story.
