Data analytics looks backward; data science looks forward.

**Direct answer:** Data analytics examines historical data to answer what happened and why. Data science builds predictive and prescriptive models to answer what will happen and what to do about it. The two overlap in tools (SQL, Python, visualization) but differ in purpose, output, and required skills. Choosing the right one depends on whether you need insight or prediction.

## Data analytics: understanding the past

Data analytics focuses on descriptive and diagnostic questions:

- What were our sales last quarter?
- Why did churn spike in October?
- Which marketing channel drove the most leads?

Typical workflow:

1. **Define the business question.**
2. **Extract data** from warehouses, logs, or CRM (SQL).
3. **Clean and transform** (pandas, SQL, dbt).
4. **Analyze and visualize** (BI tools, Jupyter, Excel).
5. **Communicate findings** to stakeholders via dashboards or reports.

Output: insights, dashboards, ad-hoc reports. Success metric: did the stakeholder make a better decision?

## Data science: predicting and prescribing

Data science builds models that generalize beyond historical data:

- Which customers will churn next month?
- What price maximizes revenue?
- Which product should we recommend to this user?

Typical workflow:

1. **Frame the problem** as prediction, classification, clustering, or optimization.
2. **Collect and engineer features** from raw data.
3. **Train and validate models** (scikit-learn, XGBoost, PyTorch).
4. **Deploy models** as APIs or batch pipelines.
5. **Monitor performance** and retrain as data drifts.

Output: models, scores, predictions, automated decisions. Success metric: model performance and business impact (lift, revenue, cost saved).

## Key differences

| Dimension | Data Analytics | Data Science |
|---|---|---|
| Primary question | What happened? Why? | What will happen? What should we do? |
| Orientation | Descriptive, diagnostic | Predictive, prescriptive |
| Time focus | Historical | Historical + future |
| Core tools | SQL, BI, spreadsheets | Python/R, ML libraries, deployment |
| Output | Reports, dashboards | Models, predictions, scores |
| Stakeholder | Business users, managers | Product, engineering, leadership |

## When to use each

**Use data analytics when:**
- You need to understand current performance.
- Stakeholders need ad-hoc answers.
- Data quality is adequate but modeling is overkill.
- The decision is one-time or infrequent.

**Use data science when:**
- You need to automate decisions at scale.
- Historical patterns should predict future outcomes.
- You have sufficient labeled data and engineering support.
- The problem recurs (churn, fraud, recommendation, pricing).

## The hybrid reality

Most mature organizations use both. Analytics monitors the business; science optimizes it. An analyst might discover churn is rising (analytics), then a data scientist builds a churn prediction model to intervene early (science). The two feed each other: analytics defines the problem, science operationalizes the solution.
