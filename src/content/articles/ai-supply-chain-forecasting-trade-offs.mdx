## The trade-offs

**Accuracy versus decision value.** <Mark>A lower mean error can hide larger tail errors</Mark>, which the demand-forecasting article treats as a metric choice, as the supply-chain metrics article argues for outcomes tied to service level, as the risk-assessment article requires residual risk to be stated.

**Complexity versus explainability.** A more complex model may be harder for planners to trust and override, which the model-explainability article treats as a trace question, as the human-in-the-loop article argues for meaningful review, as the analyst-workflow article advises on tools people actually use.

**Freshness versus stability.** Frequent retracking reacts to change but can chase noise, which the time-series article warns about non-stationarity, as the monitoring article treats retraining as a change trigger, as the model-collapse article warns about uncontrolled feedback.

**Data versus provenance.** External series help, but each input needs a source and a date, which the data-governance article requires, as FRED provides dated series, as the data-quality article warns about bad inputs.

Three consequences follow. The baseline should be a real forecast already in use, as the evaluation-rubric article requires for comparison, as the geo-experiment guidance recommends. The error metric should reflect the decision cost, as the demand-forecasting article advises. And the result should be reviewed when conditions change, as the monitoring article treats as a trigger.
