Retail is not online versus offline. It is both.

**Direct answer:** E-commerce and brick-and-mortar retail are two channels with different economics. E-commerce offers lower fixed costs, wider reach, and data-rich customer interactions, but faces shipping costs, returns, and intense price competition. Brick-and-mortar offers immediate gratification, tactile experience, and local trust, but faces high rent, staffing, and geographic limits. Most successful retailers use both channels, understanding that each serves different customer needs and that the two channels interact.

## E-commerce economics

**Advantages:**
- Lower fixed costs: no store rent, fewer staff per customer.
- Wider reach: national or global customers without physical expansion.
- Data: every click, view, and purchase is measurable.
- 24/7 availability: the store never closes.

**Challenges:**
- Shipping and returns: physical goods must move, and returns are costly.
- Price transparency: customers compare prices instantly across sites.
- Customer acquisition: online advertising costs can erode margins.
- No tactile experience: customers cannot touch or try before buying.

## Brick-and-mortar economics

**Advantages:**
- Immediate gratification: customers take products home now.
- Tactile experience: touching, trying, and testing builds confidence.
- Local trust: physical presence signals legitimacy.
- Impulse purchases: in-store discovery drives unplanned buying.

**Challenges:**
- High fixed costs: rent, utilities, staffing, inventory.
- Geographic limits: customers must travel to the store.
- Inventory management: stock must be in the right place at the right time.
- Limited hours: the store closes.

## How the channels interact

Customers do not see channels; they see a brand. They may research online and buy in-store (ROBO: research online, buy offline), or see in-store and buy online (showrooming). A physical store can serve as a pickup point for online orders, reducing shipping costs and enabling returns.

Retailers who treat channels as separate businesses miss this interaction. Inventory, pricing, and promotions should be coordinated, even if the experience differs by channel.

## The omnichannel reality

Omnichannel retail means the customer experience is consistent across channels: same prices, same promotions, same inventory visibility, same returns policy. It requires integrated systems: inventory that updates in real time, customer data that follows across channels, and fulfillment that can ship from store or warehouse.

Omnichannel is more expensive to operate than single-channel, but it meets customers where they are.

## When each channel wins

E-commerce wins for:
- Commodity products with known quality.
- Price-sensitive customers.
- Niche products with small local demand.
- Customers who value convenience over experience.

Brick-and-mortar wins for:
- High-consideration purchases (furniture, electronics, clothing).
- Products requiring testing or fitting.
- Immediate needs.
- Customers who value service and experience.
