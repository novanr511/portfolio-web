International SEO is often reduced to adding language tags, which misses the actual question: does the engine serve the right version of a page to the right audience? Hreflang is a signal that helps answer that, not a command, as the hreflang guide explains for valid clusters, as the structured-data article warns about treating markup as a guarantee.

**Direct answer:** Evaluate international SEO by checking which pages are indexed, which version is selected for each locale, and whether traffic and conversions match the intended market. Google's Search Console guidance is the primary source for diagnosis, and the hreflang guide covers the tag structure. The boundary: no structure guarantees a ranking, which the publication standard states for SEO, and hreflang does not fix thin or duplicated content, as the thin-content article warns.

## The checks

**Inventory the locales.** Which languages and regions do you actually serve, and which pages are intended for each, which the hreflang guide requires for valid clusters, as the data-governance article requires for relied-upon records? A tag pointing to a page that does not exist is an error, as the hreflang guide warns about return tags.

**Check indexing.** Are the intended pages in the index, which the Search Console guide covers, as the discovered-crawled-rendered article explains for the stages? A locale page that is not indexed cannot be served, as the indexing article treats access and indexing as distinct, as the canonicalization article explains for signals.

**Check selection.** Which version appears for a query in a given locale, which the hreflang guide treats as the purpose of the tags, as the geo-experiment guidance recommends for a comparison? Manual checks from a locale are evidence, as the measuring-GEO article advises for observable signals.

**Check outcomes.** Do traffic and conversions match the intended market, which the funnel article treats as stage measures, as the analytics article advises for observed behaviour, as the attribution article warns about credit rules?

Three consequences follow. The tags should be consistent and reciprocal, as the hreflang guide requires, as the diagnostics article advises for verification. The content should be genuinely localized, which the international-expansion article treats as more than translation, as the thin-content article warns about duplicates. And the result should be rechecked after a template change, as the monitoring article treats as a trigger.
