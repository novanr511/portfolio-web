## The starting steps

**Define the fields.** What exactly must be extracted, with what type and what rule, which the structured-output article requires for a schema, as the evaluation-rubric article demands for concrete criteria? A vague "extract the total" invites inconsistent results, as the data-governance article requires for a defined record.

**Start with one document type.** A narrow set makes errors visible, as the integration blueprint recommends for bounded experiments, as the evaluation article advises for a fixed test set.

<Trap>
Broadening too early hides failures, as the benchmarks article warns about applying results beyond conditions.
</Trap>

**Measure against a baseline.** Have a labelled sample and score field-level accuracy, which the evaluation-rubric article requires, as the data-quality article advises for a quality bar, as the dashboard article argues for metrics tied to a decision.

**Keep a human check.** <Mark>Route low-confidence or high-cost fields to a person</Mark>, which the human-in-the-loop article treats as meaningful review, as [How to Reduce Hallucinations in LLM Applications](/articles/how-to-reduce-hallucinations-in-llm-applications) warns about confident wrong output, as the decision-memo article requires for a recorded judgement.

Three consequences follow. <Mark>The schema is the contract</Mark>, which the structured-output article advises, as the data-governance article requires for provenance. <Mark>The field-level score matters more than a document-level average</Mark>, as the evaluation-rubric article requires, as the risk article requires residual risk to be stated. And the process should be reviewed when document types change, as the monitoring article treats as a trigger.

The following is hypothetical and illustrative. A team extracts totals and dates from a single invoice format.

## What document extraction cannot do

It cannot create information missing from the document, which the data-quality article treats as an upstream problem, as [How to Reduce Hallucinations in LLM Applications](/articles/how-to-reduce-hallucinations-in-llm-applications) warns about invented detail. It does not remove the need for a human check on high-cost fields, which the oversight article treats as meaningful review, as the decision-memo article requires. And it cannot guarantee accuracy across new formats, which the benchmarks article warns about, as the publication standard forbids guaranteed outcomes.

## A decision test

1. Define each field with a type and rule, as [Structured Outputs: Common Questions](/articles/structured-outputs-common-questions) requires.
2. Start with one document type, as the integration blueprint recommends.
3. Build a labelled sample, as the evaluation-rubric article requires.
4. Score field-level accuracy, as the rubric article requires.
5. Route low-confidence fields to a person, as the oversight article advises.
6. Source and date any relied-upon records, as the data-governance article requires.
7. Record the decision and review on format change, as the decision-memo article requires.
