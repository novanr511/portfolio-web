AI document extraction looks like a clean win: feed in invoices or forms, get structured fields out. The difficulty is that a plausible wrong field is worse than an obvious miss, because downstream systems act on it, which the structured-output article treats as a reliability problem, as the data-quality article warns about bad inputs, as the hallucination article warns about confident invented detail.

## The starting steps

**Define the fields.** What exactly must be extracted, with what type and what rule, which the structured-output article requires for a schema, as the evaluation-rubric article demands for concrete criteria? A vague "extract the total" invites inconsistent results, as the data-governance article requires for a defined record.

**Start with one document type.** A narrow set makes errors visible, as the integration blueprint recommends for bounded experiments, as the evaluation article advises for a fixed test set. **Measure against a baseline.** Have a labelled sample and score field-level accuracy, which the evaluation-rubric article requires, as the data-quality article advises for a quality bar, as the dashboard article argues for metrics tied to a decision.

**Keep a human check.** Route low-confidence or high-cost fields to a person, which the human-in-the-loop article treats as meaningful review, as the hallucination article warns about confident wrong output, as the decision-memo article requires for a recorded judgement.

## What document extraction cannot do

It cannot create information missing from the document, which the data-quality article treats as an upstream problem, as the hallucination article warns about invented detail.
