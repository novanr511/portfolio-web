## The mistakes

**Buying deliverables, not outcomes.** A contract that pays for a post measures activity, which the email-metrics article treats as an intermediate signal, as the funnel article advises for stage transitions. The business question is whether the content changed a decision, which the geo-experiment guidance tests with a comparison.

**No disclosure clause.** A partnership that is not disclosed <Mark>damages the credibility the channel depends on</Mark>, which the influencer-measurement article treats as an ethics and measurement question, as the online-reviews article warns about incentives that distort public evidence.

**Unclear usage rights.** Repurposing content without the right to do so creates a legal and brand risk, which the licensing discussion in the open-source article analogises to rights that must be explicit, as the term-sheet article treats terms as the substance of a deal.

**No measurement plan.** If the contract does not define how the effect will be assessed, the report will be platform-reported numbers, which the attribution article treats as credit rules, as the influencer-measurement article advises for a holdout.

Three consequences follow. The contract should name a measurement window and a baseline, as the evaluation-rubric article requires for concrete criteria, as the decision-memo article requires for a recorded judgement. It should include disclosure and rights, as the legal context requires. And it should be reviewed after the campaign, as the monitoring article advises for any maintained process.

## A worked example

The following is hypothetical and illustrative. A brand contracts an influencer for a post and a story, with no disclosure clause and a payment tied only to publication.
