A review request is a sampling instrument: the sample, wording and route chosen before sending decide which experiences can enter the public distribution. On 22 November 2026 a curation pass reduced 1,004 templated articles to a curated, restorable set behind an allowlist, a rule fixed before the archive was judged. The RAG research agent's `number_audit` ran 193 checks showing its memos matched retrieved files, a consistency result with a narrow scope. The FTC's [16 CFR §465.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465/section-465.4) prohibits compensation conditioned on review sentiment, and AAPOR's [Transparency Initiative](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/transparency-initiative/) requires disclosure of who was asked and how. Together they frame a design decision rather than a copywriting exercise.

## Where does bias enter a review request?

Bias enters when eligibility is defined, before anyone types a star rating. Choosing who receives the ask, when it arrives, what the wording invites and which destination receives the answer fixes the pool of possible reviewers. The form records what the request allowed through. Treat the request as a sampling rule and the distribution becomes an auditable record.

The request is the sampling frame. A programme that asks only customers it expects to be delighted has already selected the answer; no later change to the form can recover the missing experiences. Two neighbouring topics sit outside this article's scope. [How to Get More Customer Reviews](/articles/how-to-get-more-customer-reviews) treats timing and friction as an operations problem, and [Online Review Strategy Pitfalls](/articles/online-review-strategy-pitfalls) treats the programme as strategy. The narrower question here is the design of the ask itself: what the rule permits before a single response exists.

The destination is part of that design. One public form for everyone in the sampled group keeps every response in the same distribution. Two routes, one for customers predicted to be happy and one for the rest, produce a public record that differs systematically from the private one. The 22 November 2026 curation is a compact analogy: an allowlist decided what went live by default, and the removed files stayed restorable. A request design with one route has the same property. It is easy to state, easy to audit, and applies to every member of the group before outcomes are known.

## What does FTC 16 CFR Part 465 actually prohibit?

Part 465 prohibits buying reviews conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews and company-controlled sites presented as independent. Section 465.4 bars compensation or incentives offered in exchange for reviews expressing a particular sentiment. Section 465.5 addresses insider reviews and testimonials. The rule took effect on 21 October 2024, source-reported from the eCFR timeline and read on 22 November 2026.

The sentiment condition is the key phrase in §465.4. An incentive tied to writing a review is one thing; an incentive tied to writing a *positive* review is what the section names as an unfair or deceptive practice, and it covers negative sentiment as well. The condition can be express or implied, so a rewards scheme whose public wording never mentions a star rating can still fall inside the rule if the practical arrangement rewards one direction. That is a question about the request's structure, not its tone of voice.

Section 465.5 is equally specific about the request. Its subsections describe material connections that must be disclosed, and they explicitly exempt "generalized solicitations to purchasers" in paragraphs (b)(2)(i) and (c)(2). A general ask sent to the purchaser population is treated differently from an ask aimed at a hand-picked group whose relationship to the business is hidden. The carve-out is deliberately about how the request is designed and sent. The separate [Endorsement Guides at 16 CFR §255.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255/section-255.5) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of unexpected material connections, which is the parallel standard for what a paid endorsement may claim.

## How does a fixed sample change the distribution?

A fixed sample sets the denominator before responses arrive, so the response rate measures the same population throughout. Change the sample after seeing answers and the rate becomes a selection effect: the same reviews divided by a smaller, friendlier group will look stronger. The arithmetic is mechanical, and the worked figures below are illustrative only.

| Quantity | Status | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers in the sampled group | ASSUMED | 10,000 |
| Reviews returned | ASSUMED | 400 |
| Response rate | DERIVED | 400 / 10,000 = 4% |
| Group after positive-sentiment filter | ASSUMED | 6,000 |
| Rate on filtered group | DERIVED | 400 / 6,000, about 6.7% |

No campaign, order log or review export sat behind that table: those figures were not measured, every input is ASSUMED and each ratio is DERIVED. Nothing about the products or the customers changed between the two rate rows. Only the rule defining who was eligible changed. A team that reports the 6.7% figure without the filter has described a different population under the same label. The same failure shape appears when a measurement window is chosen after results are read; [Attribution Window as a Choice](/articles/attribution-window-as-a-choice) covers that case.

The representativeness question is separate from the count. A response rate tells you how many people answered; it says nothing about whether the 400 resemble the 10,000 on anything that matters. Compare reviewers with non-reviewers on observable fields fixed in advance — order value, product category, tenure — and record the comparison as a diagnostic. If the groups diverge, the divergence is evidence about the request as much as about the product.

## Why do incentives and insider requests need disclosure?

Disclosure attaches material context to a response, so a reader can tell an ordinary customer from someone with a stake in the outcome. Section 465.5 requires that relationship to be clear and conspicuous when an insider writes or a business disseminates the testimonial, unless it is already obvious. The obligation follows the request chain, not just the final post.

The section's reach into the request is easy to miss. A manager who solicits a review from an immediate relative or an employee, without instructing that the relationship be disclosed, is described in §465.5(c) as acting within the rule when the review appears without that disclosure. The carve-out for generalized solicitations to purchasers remains: a neutral, population-wide ask is the protected case. A targeted ask to people with a connection to the business is a different instrument, and it needs a disclosure rule written into the request itself.

Incentives need the same care. §465.4 is about sentiment-contingent compensation, while §255.5 of the Endorsement Guides is about material connections a reader would not expect. A discount offered for an honest review, disclosed in the ask, is a different design from a reward that appears only when the rating is high. Freeze the wording of both the offer and the disclosure, and keep the version with a date. A reviewer who changes the incentive midway has made a new request, with a new bias profile.

## What can AAPOR standards add to the ask?

AAPOR's standards add a reporting discipline: say who was eligible, how they were reached, what was asked, when, and what the limitations are. The [Transparency Initiative](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/transparency-initiative/) lists disclosure elements for publicly released findings, and the [AAPOR Code of Ethics](https://www.aapor.org/Standards-Ethics/AAPOR-Code-of-Ethics.aspx) sets the broader expectation of honest reporting. Neither supplies the design; both make the design describable.

An aggregate review claim is a result that inherits these obligations. A line such as "4.6 out of 5 from 400 reviewers" carries a denominator, a population, a wording and a collection period, whether or not the publisher writes them down. Under the transparency standard, the full description is the honest version: who received the request, over what dates, with which wording, and what response rate came back. AAPOR does not certify that the method was good; it asks that the method be stated so a reader can judge it.

That distinction matters for a review programme. Publishing the response rate is more informative than publishing the average alone, because a low rate tells a reader how much self-selection sits behind the average. Publishing the eligible population is more informative than publishing the raw count, because a count without a denominator cannot show which customers were never asked. The request design produces these facts at no extra cost if the rule is written down when the ask is sent rather than reconstructed later.

## How should wording and timing be frozen?

Freeze the wording, channel, timing and destination in one dated specification, then apply it to every member of the sampled group. A request that changes wording halfway through, or shifts channel by customer segment, mixes two instruments under one label. Revisions belong in a new version with a new date, not in a quiet edit.

The relevance of the [RAG agent's `number_audit`](/projects/rag-research-agent) here is methodological. Its 193 checks, read on 22 November 2026, established that generated prose matched retrieved files; they did not establish that the claims in those files were valid. A frozen request specification is checkable in the same narrow way. You can verify that the sent wording matches the approved version and that the routing rule sent every sampled customer to the same form. You cannot verify from that check that the resulting reviews describe the product accurately.

Timing needs the same discipline. A prompt sent at delivery measures first impressions; one sent at thirty days measures early durability; they answer different questions and should not be pooled without a stated rule. The 22 November 2026 curation worked because the allowlist was mechanical and dated, so a disagreement could be located in a specific row. A request log with a version, a date and an owner gives a review distribution the same property: the rule can be disputed rather than guessed at.

## What can a request design still not establish?

A well-designed request removes selection introduced by the sender; it cannot remove self-selection by the respondent. People who answer differ from those who do not, for reasons the request cannot control, so the distribution remains a record of who chose to speak under stated conditions. It offers no satisfaction survey, causal estimate or representative picture of every customer.

The limits on the local evidence are explicit. The 22 November 2026 curation changed what the site published, and the traffic outcome was not measured; the 193-check audit established consistency between a memo and its files, not validity. Neither supplies a reader response. Attaching an outcome to either would be a new claim with no observation behind it, and the same restraint applies to an illustrative review table. For the difference between a record of what people said and a campaign asset made from it, see [UGC as Evidence, Not as Content](/articles/ugc-as-evidence-not-as-content).

The decision a team can act on is narrow and durable. Define the sampled group, write one wording and one destination, disclose the connections the rule requires, log the version and the date, and report the response rate beside the average. None of those steps guarantees flattering reviews. They make the distribution interpretable, which is the only property a request can honestly promise.
