Data is only an asset if it is managed like one.

**Direct answer:** Data governance is the framework of policies, roles, and processes that ensures data is accurate, accessible, secure, and used appropriately. It defines who owns data, who can access it, how quality is maintained, and how decisions about data are made. Without governance, data becomes inconsistent, unreliable, and risky. With governance, data becomes a dependable asset for decisions, products, and compliance.

## What data governance covers

Data governance answers:

- **Ownership:** who is accountable for each dataset?
- **Access:** who can see, edit, or delete what?
- **Quality:** what standards define accurate, complete, timely data?
- **Usage:** what is allowed, and what is prohibited?
- **Compliance:** how are privacy and regulatory requirements met?
- **Issues:** how are data problems reported and resolved?

## Why governance matters

Without governance:

- **Inconsistent definitions.** "Active user" means different things to marketing, product, and finance.
- **Silos.** Data is trapped in systems and teams, inaccessible to those who need it.
- **Quality decay.** Errors propagate because no one is responsible for fixing them.
- **Compliance risk.** Personal data is mishandled, triggering fines and reputational damage.
- **Bad decisions.** Decisions are based on wrong or incomplete data.

## The governance structure

Effective governance includes:

- **Data owners:** accountable for specific datasets, responsible for quality and access decisions.
- **Data stewards:** day-to-day managers of data quality and metadata.
- **Data users:** analysts, engineers, and business staff who use data and report issues.
- **Governance council:** cross-functional leadership that sets policy and resolves conflicts.

Governance is not IT's job alone. It requires business participation because business context defines what data means.

## Data quality as governance output

Governance produces quality through:

- **Standards:** naming, formatting, and definition conventions.
- **Validation:** checks at data entry and integration points.
- **Monitoring:** alerts when quality metrics drift.
- **Remediation:** processes for fixing errors and preventing recurrence.

Quality is not a one-time cleanup. It is an ongoing discipline.

## Access and security

Governance balances access with protection:

- **Role-based access:** people see what they need for their job, no more.
- **Approval workflows:** sensitive data requires approval to access.
- **Audit logs:** who accessed what, and when.
- **Privacy controls:** personal data is handled according to regulation and expectation.

Too little access slows the business. Too much access creates risk.

## Governance is not bureaucracy

Governance is often perceived as red tape. Done well, it removes friction: trusted data means less time verifying numbers and more time making decisions. The goal is not to control every data point; it is to make the right data available to the right people at the right time.

## Implementing governance

Start small:

1. **Identify critical datasets.** Not all data needs equal governance. Start with the datasets that drive decisions.
2. **Assign owners.** Name the person accountable for each.
3. **Define standards.** Document definitions, quality rules, and access policies.
4. **Monitor and enforce.** Track compliance and address violations.
5. **Expand gradually.** Add datasets and processes as governance matures.
