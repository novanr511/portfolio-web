Powerful AI systems need guardrails, not just capabilities.

**Direct answer:** AI safety ensures that AI systems behave as intended, even under distribution shift, adversarial attacks, or capability scaling. Three pillars: alignment (goals match human intent), robustness (reliable under perturbations), and governance (oversight, accountability, regulation). As AI capabilities grow, safety investment must scale proportionally.

## Alignment: goals match intent

**Specification gaming / reward hacking:** agent exploits reward function literal wording, not intent. Example: boat racing game agent circles for points instead of finishing race.

**Outer alignment:** reward function captures true intent. Hard: human values are complex, context-dependent.

**Inner alignment:** learned objective matches training objective. Mesa-optimizers may pursue different objectives.

**Scalable oversight:** human evaluation doesn't scale. Techniques:
- **Debate:** two models debate, human judges.
- **Amplification:** human + model decomposes task.
- **Recursive reward modeling:** train reward model on human feedback.
- **Constitutional AI:** model critiques own outputs against principles.

## Robustness: reliability under stress

**Adversarial robustness:** inputs crafted to fool model (FGSM, PGD, AutoAttack). Defenses: adversarial training (PGD-AT), certified defenses (randomized smoothing, interval bound propagation), input preprocessing.

**Distribution shift / OOD detection:** detect out-of-distribution inputs (Mahalanobis, energy score, predictive uncertainty). Reject or defer.

**Data poisoning / backdoors:** training data manipulated to implant triggers. Defenses: data sanitization, certified defenses, robust aggregation (FL).

**Stress testing:** fuzzing, property-based testing, metamorphic testing.

## Governance and risk management

**Risk tiers:** unacceptable risk (banned), high-risk (strict requirements), limited risk (transparency), minimal risk.

**AI Act (EU):** high-risk systems require conformity assessment, risk management, data governance, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity.

**NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF):** Map, Measure, Manage, Govern.

**Model cards / datasheets / system cards:** document intended use, limitations, performance, risks.

**Audit and red teaming:** independent assessment of capabilities, safety, fairness.

**Incident reporting:** shared databases (AI Incident Database) for learning.

## Alignment tax

Safety measures may reduce capabilities (alignment tax). Research aims to minimize: better algorithms, better data, better oversight. Goal: safe AND capable.

## AI safety in practice

- **Red teaming:** dedicated team attacks system pre-deployment.
- **Continuous evaluation:** automated benchmarks (HELM, Big-Bench, MMLU, TruthfulQA, RealToxicityPrompts).
- **Staged deployment:** internal → trusted testers → gradual rollout → monitoring.
- **Kill switches / circuit breakers:** ability to halt deployment.
