Retention is often treated as a programme — a loyalty scheme, a win-back email, a discount — before it is treated as a measurement. The starting question is which customers stay, which leave, and why, which the retention article frames as an economic choice, as the unit-economics article treats churn as a direct input to margin. A programme without that measurement optimises the wrong behaviour.

**Direct answer:** Start by defining a cohort, measuring repeat behaviour, finding where customers leave, and testing one intervention against a baseline. The demand-generation overview explains the stages a customer passes through, and AAPOR's standards apply to any survey used to learn why. The boundary: a high retention number can reflect a loyal few, which the cohort view reveals, and no programme guarantees loyalty, as the publication standard states for commercial claims, as the loyalty article warns about points that change behaviour only briefly.

## The starting steps

**Define the cohort.** A retention rate without a cohort and a window is hard to interpret, which the retention-analytics article requires for comparable measures, as the data-governance article requires for a dated definition. **Measure repeat behaviour.** The signal is what customers do, not what they say they will do, which the NPS article warns about for intentions, as the product-analytics article recommends for observed behaviour. **Find the leak.** Where in the journey do customers stop, which the funnel article treats as a stage measure, as the churn-reduction article advises for diagnosis? A problem at onboarding is different from one at renewal, as the customer-journey article advises for evidence from real people.

**Test one intervention.** Start with a bounded change, as the integration blueprint recommends for experiments, with a baseline and a comparison, as the geo-experiment guidance advises, as the decision-memo article requires for a recorded judgement.

Three consequences follow. The measurement comes before the programme, which the retention-analytics article argues for. And the result should be reviewed after a change, as the monitoring article treats as a trigger.

## What a retention start cannot do

It cannot fix a weak product, which the value-proposition article treats as the prior decision, as the advocacy article argues for genuine value. It cannot make a survey substitute for behaviour, which the NPS article warns about for stated intentions, as the product-analytics article recommends for observed signals. And it does not guarantee loyalty, which the loyalty article treats points as unable to ensure, as the publication standard forbids guaranteed outcomes.

The counterargument that loyalty points are the obvious first step is fair for habits that already exist and misplaced as a starting move, which the loyalty article treats as a change in behaviour, as the worked example shows for diagnosis first.
