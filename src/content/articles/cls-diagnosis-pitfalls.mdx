Cumulative layout shift is a measure of how much visible content moves during a page's lifetime. It is often treated as a CSS problem, which is why teams rewrite layouts without fixing the cause: an image without reserved dimensions, a font that swaps late, or content injected after the first paint. The Core Web Vitals material defines the metric, and the font-loading article covers one common source.

**Direct answer:** Diagnose layout shift by finding which element moved and what changed its size or position, using a trace rather than a screenshot. The Core Web Vitals reference defines the score, and MDN's image guidance covers one frequent cause. The boundary: a low score does not guarantee a stable experience in conditions you did not measure, which the web-performance article warns about testing on realistic connections, as the publication standard forbids performance guarantees.

## The pitfalls

**Looking at a screenshot.** A static screenshot cannot show movement, which is why the diagnosis needs a recording or a trace, as the tracing article recommends for observing a system over time. The moment of the shift is the evidence.

**Blaming CSS first.** Layout changes are often caused by late-arriving resources — images, fonts, or ads — rather than by the stylesheet, as the image and font articles explain for reserved dimensions and display strategies, as the web-performance article advises for finding the slow layer.

**Testing on a fast connection.** Resources that arrive instantly on a developer machine may arrive late for users, which the web-performance article requires you to account for, as the mobile-first article explains for device constraints.

**Optimising the aggregate score.** A single score hides where the shift happened, which the evaluation-rubric article warns about for metrics without dimensions, as the dashboard article argues for signals tied to a decision.

Three consequences follow. The diagnosis should identify the element and the trigger, which the tracing method supports. It should be performed on a realistic connection, as the performance article advises. And the fix should be measured again, as the monitoring article treats as a review of any maintained change.

## A decision test

1. Record the shift with a trace rather than a screenshot, as the tracing article advises.
2. Identify the element that moved and the resource that triggered it.
3. Check reserved dimensions for images, as MDN's guidance requires.
4. Check font swap behaviour, as the font-loading article advises.
5. Test on a realistic connection, as the web-performance article requires.
6. Re-measure after the fix, as the monitoring article advises.
