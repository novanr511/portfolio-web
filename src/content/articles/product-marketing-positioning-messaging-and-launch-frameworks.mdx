Product marketing is the translator between what you built and why it matters.

**Direct answer:** Product Marketing (PMM) owns the bridge between product and market: positioning (who it's for, why it's different), messaging (how you talk about it), launch (how you bring it to market), and enablement (equipping sales/customer success). PMM turns features into value propositions that drive pipeline and adoption.

## Positioning: the foundation

**April Dunford's 5 components:**
1. **Competitive alternatives:** what would they do otherwise?
2. **Unique attributes:** features/capabilities only you have.
3. **Value:** outcomes those attributes enable (not features).
4. **Target market:** who cares most about this value?
5. **Market category:** mental bucket for buyers (e.g., "CRM for SMBs").

**Positioning statement template:** "For [target], who need [need], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternatives], we [differentiator]."

**Testing:** surveys (Wyner test), sales calls, win/loss analysis, A/B tests on landing pages.

## Messaging architecture

**Message hierarchy:**
1. **Tagline:** 5–7 words, memorable, differentiation.
2. **Value proposition:** one sentence, customer-centric outcome.
3. **Pillars (3–4):** key value themes (e.g., "Faster time-to-value," "Enterprise-grade security").
4. **Proof points:** stats, case studies, certifications, testimonials.
5. **Feature-to-benefit mapping:** feature → advantage → benefit.

**Persona-specific messaging:** tailor pillars/proofs for buyer (economic), user (functional), champion (emotional).

**Message testing:** message testing surveys, 5-second tests, sales call recordings, website heatmaps.

## Launch frameworks

**Launch tiers:**
- **Tier 1 (Major):** new category, major platform release. Full press, analyst briefings, events, paid, enablement blitz.
- **Tier 2 (Significant):** new module, major feature. Blog, email, webinar, sales enablement.
- **Tier 3 (Incremental):** improvements, bug fixes. Changelog, in-app notification, release notes.

**Launch phases:**
1. **Pre-launch (T-8 to T-4 weeks):** positioning locked, messaging approved, assets in production, sales enablement starts.
2. **Launch week (T-1 to T+1):** press release, blog, social, webinar, analyst briefings, customer comms.
3. **Post-launch (T+1 to T+4 weeks):** adoption tracking, customer stories, FAQ updates, competitive response.

**Launch metrics:** signups/trials, pipeline generated, feature adoption, press mentions, analyst inquiries, competitive win rate.

## Competitive differentiation

**Battlecards:** one-pager per competitor (positioning, strengths, weaknesses, trap questions, landmines, win stories).

**Differentiation types:**
- **Product:** features, architecture, integrations.
- **Company:** vision, stability, ecosystem, support.
- **Commercial:** pricing model, contract flexibility, implementation speed.

**Trap-setting:** questions only you can answer well ("How do you handle X at scale?").

## Enablement

**Sales:** pitch decks, discovery guides, objection handling, demo scripts, competitive battlecards, ROI calculators.

**CS:** onboarding playbooks, QBR templates, expansion talk tracks, churn risk signals.

**Support:** FAQ, troubleshooting guides, escalation paths.

**Launch readiness scorecard:** assets complete, sales certified, support trained, legal approved, analytics instrumented.
