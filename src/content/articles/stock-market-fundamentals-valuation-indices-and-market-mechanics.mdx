Stocks are not lottery tickets. They are partial ownership in real businesses.

**Direct answer:** The stock market is a platform where investors buy and sell shares of publicly listed companies. Share prices reflect the market's collective estimate of a company's future profitability, discounted to present value. Indices track groups of stocks to measure market performance. Understanding valuation, mechanics, and risk helps investors and business leaders interpret market movements without being misled by noise.

## What a share represents

A share is a fractional ownership stake in a company. Shareholders own a claim on future profits and assets, but they do not manage daily operations. Returns come from:

- **Capital appreciation:** selling shares at a higher price than purchased.
- **Dividends:** periodic profit distributions paid to shareholders.

## How prices are set

Stock prices are determined by supply and demand in continuous auction markets. Buyers submit bids; sellers submit asks. When bid and ask meet, a trade occurs at that price. Prices adjust instantly to new information: earnings reports, macroeconomic data, regulatory changes, or investor sentiment.

No single investor sets the price. The market price is the consensus of all participants at that moment.

## Valuation: what is a share worth?

Fundamental valuation estimates a company's intrinsic value:

- **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF):** projects future cash flows and discounts them to present value using a required rate of return.
- **Multiples:** price-to-earnings (P/E), price-to-book (P/B), and enterprise value-to-EBITDA compare the company to peers.
- **Dividend Discount Model:** values a stock based on expected future dividends.

Valuation is forward-looking and sensitive to assumptions. Small changes in growth or discount rates produce large value differences.

## Indices: measuring the market

Indices (such as the S&P 500, FTSE 100, or Nikkei 225) track a basket of stocks to represent a market or sector. They serve as:

- **Benchmarks:** comparing fund or portfolio performance.
- **Barometers:** gauging overall market sentiment.
- **Underlying assets:** for index funds and ETFs.

An index rising does not mean every stock in it rose. It reflects the weighted average movement of its components.

## Market mechanics

- **Exchanges:** regulated venues where buyers and sellers meet (physical or electronic).
- **Brokers:** intermediaries that execute trades for investors.
- **Market makers:** firms that provide liquidity by quoting both buy and sell prices.
- **Clearing and settlement:** ensuring trades are finalized and ownership transfers.

## What the stock market is not

The stock market is not the economy. Stock prices can rise while unemployment is high, or fall during GDP growth. Markets discount the future; they react to expectations, not just current conditions. Short-term movements are noisy and often driven by sentiment, not fundamentals.
