## The basics

**What is transferred.** An insurer takes on a defined risk in exchange for a premium, which the insurance entry explains for the pooling mechanism, as the unit-economics article treats the premium as a cost with a rationale, as the cash-flow-forecasting article requires for a recurring expense.

**What is retained.** Deductibles and exclusions leave part of the risk with the team, which the insurance entry describes, as [AI Risk Assessment: A Pre-Deployment Checklist](/articles/ai-risk-assessment-a-pre-deployment-checklist) requires you to state residual risk, as [Emergency Fund: How Much You Actually Need](/articles/emergency-fund-how-much-you-actually-need) treats a cash buffer as a separate layer.

**What the limit means.** A policy cap can be <Mark>below the plausible loss</Mark>, which the risk-assessment article treats as a residual exposure, as the scenario-analysis article advises for estimating severity, as the decision-memo article requires for a recorded judgement.

**What still must be done.** Controls that prevent the loss remain, which the incident-response article treats as containment, as the security-checklist article requires per-tool controls, as the data-governance article requires records for any claim.

Three consequences follow. The policy should match a real exposure, as [AI Risk Assessment: A Pre-Deployment Checklist](/articles/ai-risk-assessment-a-pre-deployment-checklist) requires, as the scenario-analysis article advises for severity. The terms should be checked with current documents, as the legal article requires a dated check, as the term-sheet article treats formal conditions as the substance. And the coverage should be reviewed when the business changes, as the monitoring article treats as a trigger.

## What insurance cannot do

It <Mark>cannot remove risk</Mark>, which the insurance entry treats as transfer, as [AI Risk Assessment: A Pre-Deployment Checklist](/articles/ai-risk-assessment-a-pre-deployment-checklist) requires residual risk to be stated. It does not replace prevention, which the incident-response article treats as containment, as the security article requires controls. And it cannot guarantee a payout, which the publication standard forbids claiming, as the legal article requires a dated check of actual terms.
