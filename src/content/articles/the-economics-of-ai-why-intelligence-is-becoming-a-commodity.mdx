Intelligence is getting cheaper. That changes who wins.

**Direct answer:** The economics of AI are shifting from building models to applying them. As foundation models become cheaper, more capable, and more accessible, raw intelligence is becoming a commodity. The value is moving up the stack: to data, distribution, domain expertise, and integration. Companies that own the customer relationship and the workflow will capture more value than companies that only sell AI models.

## The cost curve of intelligence

Training a frontier AI model once cost tens of millions of dollars. Today, capable models can be trained or fine-tuned for a fraction of that, and inference costs are falling rapidly. Open-source models, competition among providers, and hardware improvements are driving prices down.

When intelligence is cheap, it is no longer a differentiator. Every company can access similar model capabilities. The advantage shifts to how intelligence is applied.

## Where value accumulates

If models are commoditized, value moves to:

- **Proprietary data.** Models are similar; your data is unique. Companies with exclusive data can fine-tune models for their specific context.
- **Distribution.** Reaching customers is harder than building a model. Companies with existing channels can embed AI into products users already use.
- **Domain expertise.** Knowing which problem to solve, how to measure success, and where AI will fail requires industry knowledge.
- **Integration and workflow.** AI that fits into existing processes beats AI that requires new ones.
- **Trust and brand.** Users choose AI they trust, especially in high-stakes decisions like health, finance, or legal.

## The trap of selling intelligence

Companies that sell only AI capabilities—API access, model subscriptions, generic assistants—face margin compression. As models improve and prices fall, undifferentiated AI becomes a race to the bottom.

The sustainable business model is not "we have AI." It is "we use AI to solve a specific problem better than anyone else."

## What this means for strategy

- **Do not build models unless you have to.** Use existing models and fine-tune only when proprietary data justifies it.
- **Focus on the workflow.** Embed AI where decisions happen, not as a separate tool.
- **Own the customer relationship.** The company that talks to the customer captures the value.
- **Measure outcomes, not outputs.** Track revenue, retention, and cost savings—not model accuracy.
