## The metrics

**Presence on every response.** The header should appear on HTTPS responses, which MDN's reference describes, as the diagnostics article advises checking the actual response, as the observability article treats signals as evidence. A header on one endpoint only is a partial control, as [Security Headers Explained](/articles/security-headers-explained) advises for verification.

**max-age and scope.** <Mark>The lifetime and whether subdomains are included</Mark>, which MDN's reference explains, as the preload discussion treats a longer commitment, as the decision-memo article requires for a recorded judgement, as the risk-assessment article requires residual risk.

**Certificate validity.** A valid certificate must exist for HSTS to help, which the HTTPS-and-SEO article treats as a diagnostic, as the TLS discussion explains the protocol, as [Security Headers Explained](/articles/security-headers-explained) treats controls as separate.

**No mixed content.** Pages that load resources over HTTP undermine the rule, which the HTTPS-and-SEO article advises diagnosing, as [Security Headers Explained](/articles/security-headers-explained) covers related browser rules, as the diagnostic article recommends for verification.

Three consequences follow. The header should be checked in production, as the diagnostics article advises, as the observability article treats signals as evidence. The max-age should be chosen deliberately, as MDN's reference advises, as the decision-memo article requires. And the result should be reviewed after an infrastructure change, as the monitoring article treats as a trigger.
