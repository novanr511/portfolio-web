User-generated content is a record of what people said; it becomes campaign material only after a brand edits it for its own purpose. The two uses carry different evidence. A post can show that a question was asked, a complaint was made, or a product was described in a particular way, and it can support none of the claims a marketer wants to attach to it. Source-reported on 23 November 2026, Semrush counted citations across 18 tracked user-generated-content domains from January to August 2026: combined Google AI citations rose 52% while ChatGPT citations fell 44%, yet AI assistants sent just 0.14% of sessions to those sites in August. The FTC's 16 CFR §255.5 requires a clear and conspicuous disclosure of an unexpected material connection, which changes what a paid post can establish. AAPOR's Transparency Initiative asks researchers to disclose population, question wording, dates and limitations before releasing a finding. Two local artifacts hold the same line: the 22 November 2026 curation moved 1,004 templated articles to a curated set behind an allowlist, and the RAG agent's `number_audit` ran 193 checks confirming that its memos matched retrieved files. Both are records about artifacts. No traffic outcome was measured for the curation.

## What does a user post establish as evidence?

A post establishes that a person wrote something on a platform at a date the platform records. It can show the wording used, the question asked, and the context around it. On its own it establishes neither a product result nor a population frequency nor a change in anyone's decision.

A screenshot records rendered text, not the experience described in it. A review saying setup took an afternoon tells you the reviewer wrote that sentence; it says nothing about typical setup time for other buyers. Those are separate claims needing separate evidence.

One post is one observation by one account under a platform's display rules. Aggregating posts gives a distribution of what was written on that platform in that window, a fact about a self-selected set of writers. The ledger should name the platform, the window and the collection rule beside the text.

## What did the Semrush UGC study actually count?

Source-reported on 23 November 2026, Semrush counted roughly 528.3 million AI Overview citations, 263.6 million in AI Mode, 125.1 million in ChatGPT and 20.3 million in Gemini across 18 domains from January to August 2026. Those totals describe citation volume inside a tracked group, not all AI citations on the web.

Combined Google AI citations rose from 75.8 million to 115.5 million (52%); ChatGPT citations fell from 18 million to 10.1 million (44%). YouTube held about 45% of tracked AI Overview citations, Reddit roughly 52% of ChatGPT citations. Twelve of the 18 sites lost monthly ChatGPT citations, with Medium down 93% and Pinterest down 92% (source-reported, 23 November 2026).

Every figure carries the study's caveat. The 18 domains are a tracked set, so a share is concentration within that group, not a market share, and the analysis groups by domain rather than verifying each cited page is user-generated. The numbers describe where answer systems drew material in eight months of US prompt data, not customer opinion or a result from your own site.

## Why is a citation share different from a referral rate?

A citation share is a fraction of source labels inside a tracked set; a referral is a request carrying a referrer. In August 2026 AI assistants sent 0.14% of sessions to those 18 domains, while direct traffic was 76.7% and organic search 15.8% (source-reported, 23 November 2026).

An answer can describe a product using a forum thread and never send a click, so a citation share can move without a referral in the log. Semrush states that its citation and referral analyses cover different products and should not be combined into a click-through rate. Treating a share as a traffic forecast skips that boundary.

Being named, being cited and being visited are three events with different checks. [What AI Citations Actually Prove and What They Do Not](/articles/what-ai-citations-actually-prove) treats the attribution trace; the narrower claim here is that a post can evidence a description without evidencing a visit.

## What does the disclosure rule change about a paid post?

Under 16 CFR §255.5, a connection that might materially affect an endorsement's weight must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously when the audience would not expect it. Payment, free or discounted products, and early access all count. A disclosed post is still an interested account, but the reader can place it correctly.

Section 255.1 adds that endorsements must reflect honest opinion or experience and may not be reworded to distort it. Section 255.5 gives examples of an influencer given an expensive tool and of reviewers receiving a product plus cash, where a free-product disclosure alone is inadequate. These are rules about presenting claims, not campaign measurements.

For evidence purposes the consequence is a column in the ledger. A post with a material connection is an endorsement with a disclosed interest; one written without it is a different observation. Neither becomes proof that the product performed as described, and neither gains a denominator. The programme itself is treated in [Customer Advocacy and User-Generated Content](/articles/customer-advocacy-and-user-generated-content).

## What makes an observation usable as evidence at all?

AAPOR's Transparency Initiative requires members to disclose collection method, sponsor, question wording, population, sampling, dates, completed responses and limitations alongside their findings. The standard is disclosure, not a quality rating: a disclosed method lets a reader judge a claim, and an undisclosed one leaves its weight unknowable.

AAPOR states that it makes no judgment about the rigour of disclosed methods. The disclosure is what travels with an observation: a forum thread captured on a stated date under a stated search rule can be rechecked, while a thread quoted without either is an anecdote. Those fields matter for survey reports and for user comments alike.

Applying the standard to UGC does not require inventing a survey. It requires writing down how the material was found: which platform, which query or feed, which window, whether incentives were offered, and what was excluded. Where those fields are absent, the honest label is an observation with an unknown collection rule. [Online Review Strategy Pitfalls](/articles/online-review-strategy-pitfalls) deals with the operational side of review collection; this article stops at what a collected post can support.

## What can the local artifacts prove?

The 22 November 2026 curation moved 1,004 templated articles to a curated set behind an allowlist; traffic was not measured. The RAG agent's `number_audit` ran 193 checks showing its memos matched retrieved files. Both are claims about internal structure and consistency, not about what a reader did afterwards.

The audit compares numbers in generated prose with retrieved files, and a figure with no match fails. A pass means the memo agrees with its files and would have reproduced a wrong figure faithfully had one sat there. The 193 checks establish consistency; the agent's build log separates that from validity.

The curation is a decision record rather than a test. It changed what goes live by default and left the removed pages restorable, which is a structural fact about the site as of 22 November 2026. The reasoning is recorded in [Why I killed 966 articles](/articles/why-i-killed-966-articles). Like the audit, it supports a claim about an artifact. Neither artifact supplies a reader response, and attaching one to either would be a new, unmeasured claim.

## Which conclusions should be refused?

Refuse frequency claims from a single platform, causal claims from a timing coincidence, and reach claims from impressions. Keep four columns beside each figure: observed, source-reported with its date, assumed, and no measurement. The fourth column protects the argument where the evidence stops short.

A spike after a launch does not show the launch caused it; posts may be queued or amplified. A hundred reviews on one platform say nothing about the share of buyers who reviewed, because the platform holds no list of buyers. A source-reported share from 18 domains says nothing about a domain outside that set.

None of this makes UGC useless. It makes the claim narrower and the source identifiable. A team that records what the text said, when it was collected, and what comparison would be needed for a stronger claim has an evidence record that survives review. A team that quotes the text as a customer verdict has a marketing asset whose accuracy nobody can check later.

## What decision does an evidence ledger support?

Treat each post as one observation with a source, a date and a collection rule. Decide which claim it can carry before using it in a report, and leave the causal column empty where no comparison exists. That makes a UGC record usable as evidence without turning it into campaign copy.

User voices matter as first-hand text: a person described a product under conditions you can name. They become misleading when a quotation stands in for a measured outcome. Without a holdout or a defined cohort, the deliverable is the ledger — observation, provenance and limit — rather than a percentage dressed as a finding.

One boundary is worth keeping in view at the end. A post can be evidence of a description and still be volatile: the same platform can surface different material on the next check. That stability question belongs in [Why a Single Citation Check Misleads](/articles/why-a-single-citation-check-misleads). Here the conclusion stays narrow and firm — user-generated content is evidence of what was said, and the brand decides whether to use it as material.
