Culture is often introduced through perks and slogans — free lunches, values on a wall. The organisational-culture literature treats it as shared values and behaviours that shape how work gets done, which means it forms through decisions, not declarations. A beginner's first task is to look at what the team rewards and tolerates.

**Direct answer:** Culture is observed in what gets rewarded, what gets tolerated, and how disagreements are handled. The organizational-culture overview provides the academic context, and O*NET's task descriptions help define the work a team actually performs. The boundary: a cultural claim is not evidence of how a team behaves, which the evaluation-rubric article argues for observable criteria, as the engineering-management article treats culture as built by decisions.

## How it forms

**What gets rewarded.** If speed is praised while quality problems are ignored, the team learns that speed wins, as the engineering-management article treats metrics as signals. The reward may be informal, which makes it more powerful than a stated value, as the behavioural-finance article warns about incentives.

**What gets tolerated.** A missed deadline that nobody discusses teaches that deadlines are optional, which the project-management context in the team article implies. The response to the first small violation sets the norm, as the incident-response article argues for acting on early signals.

**How disagreement works.** A team where people can challenge a decision without penalty makes better decisions, which the human-in-the-loop article treats as a condition for surfacing problems. The alternative is agreement in the room and doubt afterwards, as the internal-communications article warns about channels that carry only good news.

Three consequences follow. Culture is shaped by leadership behaviour, which the management article treats as the substance of culture. It is visible in artefacts — how meetings run, how feedback is given — which the documentation article argues for putting guidance where work happens. And it changes when incentives change, as the unit-economics article treats measurement as a driver of behaviour.

## A worked example

The following is hypothetical and illustrative. A new manager joins a team that says it values quality but ships with failing tests.

Three checks decide what to do. First, what is rewarded, as the management article asks for the substance of culture? ## How to observe culture without a survey

Culture can be read from ordinary artefacts. Look at which meetings are cancelled, whose suggestions are adopted, and what happens after a mistake, which the organisational-culture overview treats as expressions of shared values rather than declarations.
