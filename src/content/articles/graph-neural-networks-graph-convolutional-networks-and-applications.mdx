Many real-world systems are graphs. GNNs learn from structure.

**Direct answer:** Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) operate on graph-structured data (nodes, edges, features) by passing messages along edges to learn node, edge, or graph-level representations. Core architectures: GCN (spectral convolution), GraphSAGE (inductive sampling), GAT (attention-based aggregation). Applications: recommendation, fraud detection, molecular property prediction, traffic forecasting, social network analysis.

## Graph representation

A graph G = (V, E) with nodes V, edges E, node features X ∈ R^(N×D), adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^(N×N). May include edge features E_f.

## Message passing framework

GNNs follow a message passing neural network (MPNN) paradigm:

1. **Message:** `m_v = Σ_{u∈N(v)} M(h_u, h_v, e_uv)` — aggregate neighbor info.
2. **Update:** `h_v' = U(h_v, m_v)` — update node representation.

After K layers, node embeddings capture K-hop neighborhood structure.

## Core architectures

**GCN (Graph Convolutional Network):** spectral convolution approximated by localized first-order filter. H^(l+1) = σ(D^(-1/2) A D^(-1/2) H^(l) W^(l)). Transductive (requires full graph during training).

**GraphSAGE:** inductive. Samples fixed-size neighborhood, applies aggregator (mean, LSTM, pooling). Learns aggregator function, generalizes to unseen nodes.

**GAT (Graph Attention Network):** attention-weighted aggregation. α_ij = softmax(LeakyReLU(a^T [Wh_i || Wh_j])). Learns edge importance. Multi-head attention.

**GraphTransformer / Graphormer:** transformer on graphs with structural encodings. Strong on molecular graphs.

**Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNN):** general framework unifying GNN variants.

## Training objectives

- **Node classification:** semi-supervised (GCN, GraphSAGE).
- **Link prediction:** predict missing edges (dot product of embeddings).
- **Graph classification:** readout (sum/mean/max pooling + MLP).
- **Node embedding:** unsupervised (DeepWalk, Node2Vec) or contrastive (GraphCL, GRACE).

## Applications

- **Recommendation:** user-item bipartite graph (PinSage, LightGCN).
- **Fraud detection:** transaction graph, anomalous subgraphs.
- **Drug discovery:** molecular property prediction (Graphormer, Grover).
- **Traffic forecasting:** spatial-temporal GNNs (STGCN, DCRNN).
- **Knowledge graphs:** RGCN, CompGCN for relation prediction.

## Challenges

- **Scalability:** mini-batch sampling (GraphSAGE, Cluster-GCN), distributed training.
- **Oversmoothing:** deep GNNs converge to similar embeddings. Residual connections, jumping knowledge.
- **Heterophily:** neighbors have different labels. H2GCN, GPR-GNN.
- **Dynamic graphs:** temporal GNNs (TGN, DyRep).

## Further Reading

- Thomas N. Kipf and Max Welling, 2017. [Semi-Supervised Classification with Graph Convolutional Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907) — The graph convolution that started modern graph neural networks.
- William L. Hamilton, Rex Ying, and Jure Leskovec, 2020. [Inductive Representation Learning on Large Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216) — Neighbour sampling and aggregation for graphs larger than one batch.
- Petar Veličković et al., 2018. [Graph Attention Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10903) — Attention-weighted message passing, the basis of most modern GNN architectures.
- Steffen Rendle et al., 2012. [Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108) — Graph collaborative filtering that propagates over the user-item graph.
