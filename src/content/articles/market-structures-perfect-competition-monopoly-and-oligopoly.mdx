Not all markets are the same. Structure determines conduct.

**Direct answer:** Market structure describes the number of firms, the nature of products, and the ease of entry in a market. The main types—perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and monopoly—determine how much pricing power firms have, how much they innovate, and how much consumers pay. Understanding structure helps businesses anticipate competitive behavior and regulators assess market power.

## Perfect competition: the theoretical benchmark

Conditions:

- Many buyers and sellers.
- Identical (homogeneous) products.
- Free entry and exit.
- Perfect information.

Firms are price takers: they accept the market price. In the long run, economic profit is zero because entry erases excess returns. Rare in reality, but useful as a benchmark for efficiency.

## Monopolistic competition: differentiation rules

Conditions:

- Many sellers.
- Differentiated products (branding, quality, features).
- Relatively free entry and exit.

Examples: restaurants, clothing brands, hair salons. Firms have some pricing power due to differentiation, but entry limits long-run profits. Competition is on quality, marketing, and location as much as price.

## Oligopoly: a few dominate

Conditions:

- Few large firms dominate the market.
- High barriers to entry (capital, regulation, network effects).
- Products may be homogeneous (steel) or differentiated (automobiles, smartphones).

Firms are interdependent: one firm's pricing or output decision affects others. Outcomes range from collusion (cartels, usually illegal) to price wars to tacit coordination. Game theory is the main tool for analyzing oligopoly behavior.

## Monopoly: one seller

Conditions:

- Single seller of a product with no close substitutes.
- High barriers to entry (patents, control of key resources, economies of scale, regulation).

A monopoly is a price maker: it can set price above marginal cost and earn sustained economic profit. However, it may produce less and charge more than a competitive market, creating deadweight loss. Natural monopolies (where one firm is most efficient due to high fixed costs, like utilities) are often regulated.

## Barriers to entry: what protects incumbents

- **Economies of scale:** larger firms have lower average costs.
- **Control of essential resources.**
- **Legal barriers:** patents, licenses, regulations.
- **Network effects:** value increases with user count.
- **Strategic behavior:** limit pricing, exclusive contracts.

## Why structure matters

Market structure affects:

- **Prices:** more competition generally means lower prices.
- **Innovation:** monopolies may invest in R&D due to profits, but competitive markets may innovate faster to survive.
- **Consumer choice:** monopolistic competition offers variety; monopoly offers uniformity.
- **Regulatory scrutiny:** dominant firms face antitrust investigation.
