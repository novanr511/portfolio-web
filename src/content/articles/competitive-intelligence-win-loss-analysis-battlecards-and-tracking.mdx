Ignoring competitors is not a strategy. Intelligence is.

**Direct answer:** Competitive Intelligence (CI) systematically collects, analyzes, and disseminates information about competitors, markets, and customers to support strategic decisions. Core practices: win/loss analysis (why we win/lose), battlecards (actionable competitive positioning), continuous tracking (pricing, product, messaging, strategy), and dissemination (alerts, briefings, war rooms). CI turns market noise into actionable signal.

## Win/loss analysis

**Purpose:** understand root causes of won/lost deals. Not "who won" but "why."

**Process:**
1. **Sample selection:** recent closed-won/closed-lost (last 30–90 days), stratified by segment, rep, competitor.
2. **Interview guide:** decision criteria, evaluation process, strengths/weaknesses, pricing, relationship, product gaps.
3. **Interviewer:** neutral party (not the rep). 20–30 min phone/video.
4. **Analysis:** code themes, quantify frequency, segment by competitor/segment/rep.
4. **Report:** top 3 win reasons, top 3 loss reasons, competitive gaps, action items.

**Cadence:** monthly pulse, quarterly deep-dive, annual strategic review.

**Metrics:** win rate by competitor, loss reason frequency, average sales cycle vs competitor, discount depth vs competitor.

## Battlecards

**Battlecard anatomy (one-pager per competitor):**
- **Positioning:** their claim vs your counter.
- **Strengths:** where they win (feature, price, brand).
- **Weaknesses:** where they lose (gaps, debt, support).
- **Trap questions:** questions only you win ("How do you handle X at scale?").
- **Landmines:** their FUD tactics, how to deflect.
- **Win stories:** 1–2 sentence proof points.
- **Objection handlers:** "They say X. You say Y."
- **Pricing intelligence:** list price, discount depth, contract terms.

**Battlecard types:**
- **Strategic (1:1):** deep, for top competitors.
- **Tactical (1:few):** for tier-2 competitors.
- **Quick reference:** one-page cheat sheet for reps.

**Maintenance:** quarterly review, win/loss feedback, product marketing owns, sales enables.

## Competitor tracking

**Signals to monitor:**
- **Product:** launches, features, pricing changes, API/docs updates.
- **Marketing:** messaging shifts, campaign launches, content themes, SEO/SEM keywords.
- **Sales:** hiring (sales/engineering), customer wins/losses, partner announcements.
- **Financial:** funding, M&A, earnings calls, investor decks.
- **Organizational:** leadership changes, layoffs, office openings/closings.

**Sources:**
- **Public:** website, blog, press, SEC filings, job boards, Glassdoor, G2/Capterra, social.
- **Semi-private:** analyst briefings, customer calls, partner channels, events.
- **Tools:** Crayon, Klue, Kompyte, Contify, Owler, Owler, Google Alerts, Feedly.

**Cadence:** daily alerts, weekly digest, monthly strategic brief, quarterly competitive landscape report.

## Dissemination and action

**Audiences and formats:**
- **Sales:** battlecards, Slack alerts, weekly competitive standup.
- **Product:** feature gaps, roadmap input, quarterly competitive landscape.
- **Marketing:** messaging updates, campaign angles, SEO keywords.
- **Leadership:** quarterly competitive landscape, strategic threat assessment.

**War rooms:** pre-launch (ours or theirs), major competitive threat, M&A activity.

## Ethics and legal

- **Legal:** public sources only. No theft, trespass, misrepresentation, NDA violation.
- **Ethical:** no deception, no recruiting for secrets, no hacking.
- **Compliance:** document sources, retain records, train team annually.
