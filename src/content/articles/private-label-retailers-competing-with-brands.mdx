The store brand is not a second choice. It is a strategy.

**Direct answer:** Private label products are brands owned by retailers and sold exclusively in their stores. They compete with national brands on price, quality, and availability. Private labels offer retailers higher margins, customer loyalty, and differentiation, but they require investment in product development, supply chain, and marketing. The economics work when the retailer can offer comparable quality at a lower price—or a unique product nobody else has.

## What private label is

Private label (also called store brand or own brand) is a product developed, branded, and sold by a retailer rather than a manufacturer. Examples include supermarket house brands, pharmacy store brands, and retailer-exclusive product lines.

Unlike national brands (P&G, Nestlé, Nike), private labels are tied to the retailer. Customers buy them because they trust the store, not because they know the manufacturer.

## Why retailers launch private labels

**Higher margins.** Retailers capture the manufacturer's margin by cutting out the brand owner. A private label product can be priced lower than the national brand and still earn more for the retailer.

**Differentiation.** If every store sells the same national brands, price is the only differentiator. Private label creates unique products that customers can only get at that retailer.

**Customer loyalty.** Customers who like a store brand return to that store for it. The brand becomes a reason to choose the retailer over competitors.

**Supply chain control.** Retailers can work directly with manufacturers, control specifications, and respond faster to trends.

## The economics

Private label economics depend on:

- **Development cost.** Product formulation, packaging, testing, and compliance.
- **Manufacturing.** Often outsourced to third-party manufacturers (contract producers).
- **Marketing.** Retailers must build awareness without the national brand's advertising.
- **Shelf space.** Private labels compete for the same limited space as national brands.
- **Pricing.** Typically priced 15–30% below national brands, with higher retailer margins.

The business works when volume and margin outweigh development and marketing costs.

## Risks and challenges

**Quality perception.** Customers may assume store brands are inferior. Quality must match or exceed national brands to overcome this.

**Manufacturer relationships.** National brands may reduce support or co-op advertising if the retailer competes with them directly.

**Complexity.** More SKUs, more suppliers, more quality control. Each private label product adds operational complexity.

**Brand risk.** A private label failure reflects on the retailer, not an external brand.

## When private label works

Private label succeeds when:

- The category is commoditized (staples, paper, cleaning, basic food).
- The retailer has strong customer trust.
- The retailer controls shelf space and can give private label prominent placement.
- Margins on national brands are thin.
- The retailer can achieve sufficient volume.

Private label struggles when:

- Brand loyalty is strong (luxury, fashion, premium electronics).
- The category requires specialized expertise the retailer lacks.
- Volume is too low to justify development costs.

## The evolution of private label

Modern private labels are not just cheap alternatives. Retailers launch premium private labels, niche products, and exclusive lines that compete on quality and innovation, not just price. Some private labels have become brands in their own right, with dedicated followings.
