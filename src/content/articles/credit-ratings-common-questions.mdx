A credit rating compresses a view of default risk into a grade, which is useful for comparison and easy to over-read. The bond-rating literature treats a rating as a structured opinion, not a measurement of a company's health, as the credit-spreads article explains for how the market prices risk separately, as the fixed-income article covers the relationship between yield and price.

**Direct answer:** A rating expresses an agency's view of an issuer's ability to meet obligations, with a scale and a methodology that should be read alongside the grade. ## Common questions

**What does the grade mean?** It is an ordinal opinion about creditworthiness, which the bond-rating entry explains for the scale, as the credit-spreads article treats the market's pricing as a separate signal. A higher grade suggests lower perceived default risk, not a guarantee of payment.

**Can ratings disagree?** Yes, because agencies use different methodologies and update on different schedules, which the comparables article analogises to selection and adjustment differences, as the benchmark article warns about comparing results from different conditions.

**Do ratings change?** They can be upgraded or downgraded when an agency revises its view, which the monitoring article treats as a change signal, as the debt article explains how a covenant breach can precede a downgrade.

**Are ratings enough?** No. They do not capture the specific terms of a bond, its seniority, or its price, which the fixed-income article explains for how yield and price relate, as the term-sheet article treats terms as the substance of a deal.

The grade should be read with the outlook and the date, which the data-governance article requires for relied-upon figures, as the market-structure article advises for dated data. It should be paired with the yield, which the credit-spreads article treats as the market's own assessment. And the issuer's financials still matter, which the company-analysis article treats as the prior step, as the earnings-quality article warns about clean-looking numbers.
