Product-led growth is often described as the product selling itself, which makes it sound like removing sales rather than changing the path to value. The actual mechanism is a loop: the product exposes value early, users reach an aha moment without a sales call, and satisfied users bring others, which the product-led-growth source frames as a growth model, as the funnel entry describes the stages a user passes through, as the freemium article treats the free boundary as a decision.

**Direct answer:** Product-led growth means the product is the primary path to acquisition, activation, and expansion, with the loop measured at each stage. Product-Led Growth is the primary source, and the purchase-funnel entry covers the stages. The boundary: self-serve does not remove the work of onboarding, support, or measurement, which the activation-email article treats as a cost, as the dashboard article argues for metrics tied to decisions, as the publication standard forbids guaranteed growth.

## The loop

**Acquisition through the product.** A user arrives through a shared artefact, a free tier, or a template, which the viral-marketing entry describes for word-of-mouth growth, as the referral article treats incentives as a design, as the freemium article argues for a defined free boundary.

**Activation without a sales call.** The user reaches value on their own, which the activation-email article treats as a sequence that must be built, as the onboarding article advises for a path to value, as the evaluation-rubric article requires for a concrete aha moment.

**Retention and expansion.** The product must remain useful, which the retention article treats as an economic choice, as the PLG loops article describes expansion, as the cohort method advises for grouping users by start period.

**Measurement at each stage.** The loop is only as good as its measured transitions, which the funnel entry describes, as the dashboard article advises for metrics tied to decisions, as the evaluation-rubric article requires for concrete criteria.

Three consequences follow. PLG is a go-to-market choice, not the absence of marketing, which the go-to-market article treats as a process, as the product-marketing article advises for positioning. ## What product-led growth cannot do

It cannot fix a product that does not deliver value, which the value-proposition article treats as prior, as the product-marketing article advises for positioning.
