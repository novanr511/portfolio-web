Great ideas need capital to scale.

**Direct answer:** Venture capital (VC) and private equity (PE) are investment models that provide capital to companies in exchange for equity ownership. VC typically funds early-stage, high-growth startups with uncertain outcomes; PE typically invests in more mature companies, often taking control to restructure or grow them. Both seek exits—through acquisition or IPO—that generate returns far above public markets.

## Venture capital: funding early-stage growth

VC firms raise funds from limited partners (pension funds, endowments, wealthy individuals) and invest in startups. Key characteristics:

- **High risk, high return:** most portfolio companies fail; a few successes must cover all losses and generate fund returns.
- **Stage focus:** seed, Series A, B, C, etc., as the company matures.
- **Active involvement:** VCs often take board seats and advise on strategy, hiring, and follow-on funding.
- **Dilution:** founders give up ownership in exchange for capital and expertise.

## Private equity: funding mature companies

PE firms invest in established companies, often buying controlling stakes. Strategies include:

- **Buyouts:** acquiring a company, often with debt (leveraged buyout), to improve operations and sell later.
- **Growth equity:** minority investments in profitable companies seeking expansion capital.
- **Distressed investing:** buying troubled companies at a discount and turning them around.

PE investors focus on operational efficiency, cost reduction, and cash flow—unlike VC, which focuses on growth potential.

## What investors look for

- **Large addressable market:** the opportunity must be big enough to generate fund-level returns.
- **Strong team:** execution matters more than the initial idea.
- **Scalability:** the business can grow revenue faster than costs.
- **Competitive advantage:** barriers to entry protect margins.
- **Clear exit path:** acquisition or IPO within 5–10 years.

## What founders give up

- **Equity:** ownership percentage decreases with each funding round.
- **Control:** investors may demand board seats, veto rights, and input on major decisions.
- **Speed:** funded companies face pressure to grow quickly, sometimes at the expense of sustainability.

## The exit

Returns are realized when:

- **Acquisition:** another company buys the startup.
- **IPO:** shares are sold to the public on a stock exchange.
- **Secondary sale:** shares are sold to another private investor.

Without an exit, investors cannot realize returns, and founders cannot cash out.

## Risks

- **VC:** most investments fail; returns depend on a small number of outliers.
- **PE:** leverage increases risk; operational turnarounds may fail; market conditions can block exits.
