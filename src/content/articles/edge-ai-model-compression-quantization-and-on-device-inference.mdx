Cloud is not always the answer. Edge brings intelligence to the device.

**Direct answer:** Edge AI deploys ML models directly on devices (phones, IoT, sensors, vehicles) instead of relying on cloud inference. Benefits: low latency, privacy (data stays local), offline capability, bandwidth savings, cost reduction. Core techniques: quantization (INT8/INT4), pruning, knowledge distillation, efficient architectures. Frameworks: TensorFlow Lite, PyTorch Mobile, ONNX Runtime, Core ML, TensorRT.

## Why Edge AI

- **Latency:** sub-ms inference for real-time (AR, robotics, autonomous driving).
- **Privacy:** raw data never leaves device (face unlock, health monitoring).
- **Bandwidth:** no video/audio upload to cloud.
- **Availability:** works offline (maps, translation, voice assistants).
- **Cost:** no cloud GPU inference bills.

## Model compression techniques

**Quantization:** reduce weight/activation precision.
- **Post-training quantization (PTQ):** INT8 dynamic, static, full integer. No retraining. 4x size reduction, 2-4x speedup.
- **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):** simulate quantization during training. Recovers accuracy. INT4/INT2 emerging.
- **Mixed precision:** sensitive layers FP16/INT8, others INT4/INT8.

**Pruning:** remove redundant weights.
- **Unstructured:** zero individual weights (sparse). Needs sparse hardware support.
- **Structured:** remove channels/filters/heads (dense). Compatible with dense kernels.
- **Iterative pruning:** prune → fine-tune → repeat.

**Knowledge distillation:** train small student to mimic large teacher. Soft labels (logits) + hard labels. Temperature scaling. Self-distillation (teacher = student ensemble).

**Efficient architectures:** MobileNetV3, EfficientNet-Lite, GhostNet, ShuffleNet, MobileViT, FastViT. Designed for mobile NPUs.

## Quantization details

**INT8 symmetric:** int8 weights, uint8/INT8 activations. Per-tensor or per-channel scale.
**INT4:** 4-bit weights, 8-bit activations. 2x smaller than INT8. Requires GEMM support (ARM NEON, Apple Neural Engine, Qualcomm Hexagon).
**Mixed precision:** sensitive layers (first/last, attention) higher precision.

**Calibration:** static quantization needs representative dataset for activation ranges. ~100-500 samples.

## Frameworks and deployment

| Framework | Platform | Quantization | Hardware Acceleration |
|---|---|---|---|
| TensorFlow Lite | Android, iOS, Linux | PTQ, QAT | GPU, NNAPI, Hexagon, Core ML delegate |
| PyTorch Mobile | Android, iOS | PTQ, QAT | Metal, NNAPI |
| ONNX Runtime | Cross-platform | PTQ, QAT | CPU, GPU, TensorRT, Core ML, NNAPI |
| Core ML | iOS/macOS | PTQ, QAT | Neural Engine |
| TensorRT | NVIDIA GPU | PTQ, QAT | Tensor Cores |
| TVM / Apache TVM | Cross-platform | Auto-tuning | CPU, GPU, NPU |

## Deployment workflow

1. Train model (PyTorch/TensorFlow).
2. Export to ONNX / SavedModel.
3. Quantize (PTQ or QAT).
4. Convert to target format (TFLite, ONNX, Core ML).
5. Benchmark on target device (latency, memory, accuracy).
6. Profile: Nsight, Instruments, Perfetto.
7. Iterate: prune → distill → quantize.

## Challenges

- **Accuracy drop:** aggressive quantization hurts. QAT + mixed precision mitigates.
- **Operator support:** custom ops may not quantize. Fallback to FP32.
- **Hardware fragmentation:** diverse NPUs, drivers, APIs.
- **Model size vs accuracy:** Pareto frontier.
- **Security:** model extraction, adversarial attacks on device.

## Further Reading

- Song Han et al., 2015. [Deep Compression: Compressing Deep Neural Networks with Pruning, Trained Quantization and Huffman Coding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.00149) — The pruning-and-quantisation pipeline behind most on-device compression.
- Jacob et al., 2018. [Quantization and Training of Neural Networks for Efficient Integer-Arithmetic-Only Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05877) — Integer-only inference and why it needs a quantisation-aware training procedure.
- Geoffrey Hinton, Oriol Vinyals, and Jeff Dean, 2015. [Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531) — Soft-label distillation, the basis of most model compression work.
- Mingxing Tan and Quoc V. Le, 2019. [EfficientNet: Rethinking Model Scaling for Convolutional Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11946) — Compound scaling of depth, width, and resolution.
