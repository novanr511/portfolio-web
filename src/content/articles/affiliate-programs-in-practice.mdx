Affiliate programmes are often measured by the sales they report, which mixes new demand with credit assigned to existing demand. The Wikipedia entry describes affiliate marketing, and the word-of-mouth entry covers how recommendations spread. The practice is about knowing what the programme actually caused.

**Direct answer:** Run an affiliate programme with a defined outcome, a fair commission, and a check on incrementality. The sources describe the channel; the measurement rule is a synthesis.

**Delta:** This article adds a claim-status table and a worked review, separating documented channel facts from local choices about credit.

## The mechanism

Three points matter:

<Figure src="/images/articles/inline/affiliate-programs-in-practice-1.png" alt="A flow: define the outcome, set commission terms, measure with a comparison, then review partner quality." caption="Conceptual: credit is assigned, but incrementality requires a comparison." />

- **The channel is performance-based.** The affiliate entry describes paying for referred outcomes. That makes the credit rule central to the programme.
- **Recommendations spread socially.** The word-of-mouth entry describes how people influence each other. An affiliate's value is often their credibility, not just their reach.
- **Credit is not cause.** A tracked sale can come from a customer who would have bought anyway. The comparison is the causal-inference correction.

The sequence is: define the outcome, set terms, measure with a comparison, then review. The terms are local judgement, so they are `inferred`; the channel is `source-reported`.

## A worked review

*Illustrative.* An affiliate reports a strong return based on tracked sales.

Three checks decide. First, does the tracking window credit purchases that would have happened without the referral? Second, is the commission consistent with the value of a genuinely new customer? Third, is there a period or region without affiliate activity that can serve as a comparison?

The outcome is a qualified assessment. The scenario is hypothetical; the method is the contribution.

## Why terms shape behaviour

A commission that pays for every signup will attract partners who optimise for signups, including ones that never become customers. A commission tied to a retained customer changes the incentive. The terms are therefore a product decision, not an administrative detail, and they should be reviewed against the outcome the programme wants.

## Why partner quality varies

Two partners with the same audience size can produce very different results because their credibility differs. Reviewing partners on the quality of referred customers, not just volume, is the correction. It also reveals which partners are worth supporting with better materials.

## Why credibility matters more than volume

An affiliate with a small, trusting audience can be more valuable than one with a large indifferent one. The mechanism is the same as word of mouth: the recommendation works because the source is trusted. Selecting on reach alone misses this.

A programme cannot create demand for a product that does not convert. It also cannot remove the need to check fraud, which grows with the value of the commission.

The measurement should be reviewed on a schedule. A channel that was incremental at one point can become a source of cannibalised sales as the audience overlaps more with existing customers, and a comparison is the only way to see that change.

A partner with strong credibility is worth more than one with a large list, which is why the review should include the quality of referred customers rather than only their count.

A final issue is the relationship between the programme and the brand. An affiliate who misrepresents the product may produce sales that are later refunded, and the cost of that damage does not appear in the commission report.

The programme should therefore include rules about claims and a way to review them. That is a governance decision, not a detail of tracking, and it protects the credibility the channel depends on.

The incrementality check should be repeated after the audience grows, because an overlap that was small at launch can become large later.

A commission structure that rewards volume alone will attract the wrong partners, which is a design choice the programme owner controls.

The record should state which partners were reviewed and what the review found, so the next cycle starts from evidence.

The comparison period should match the affiliate's typical conversion window to avoid a false result.

## What it cannot do

It cannot guarantee incremental sales. It does not remove the need to check fraud. And it cannot replace a value proposition that converts the referred customer.

The counterargument that tracked sales are the whole result is fair as a record and incomplete as a cause.

## A decision test

1. Define the outcome.
2. Set fair terms.
3. Check the tracking window.
4. Use a comparison.
5. Review partner quality.
6. Record the decision and recheck date.
