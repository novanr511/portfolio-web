## The file and its limits

Before naming a driver of attrition, I ranked all 18 categorical and ordinal fields in the file. That changed the headline. The source is the [IBM HR Attrition mirror](https://github.com/mrc03/IBM-HR-Analytics-Employee-Attrition-Performance/blob/master/WA_Fn-UseC_-HR-Employee-Attrition.csv), 1,470 records in a synthetic teaching dataset created by IBM. It contains no real employees. Figures are computed by `scripts/build_data_analytics_demos.py`.

## Ranking every split

Overall attrition is **16.12%**. Each field was measured by the spread between its highest and lowest group rate, and by the best single-category gap from the overall rate of 16.12%. The five widest are shown; JobRole remains the widest of all 18.

| Field | Spread across categories | Best single-category gap |
|---|---:|---:|
| JobRole | 37.26 pp | Sales Representative, 23.64 pp |
| JobInvolvement | 24.71 pp | level 1, 17.61 pp |
| JobLevel | 21.62 pp | level 4, 11.41 pp |
| OverTime | 20.09 pp | Yes, 14.41 pp |
| TrainingTimesLastYear | 18.55 pp | 0 trainings, 11.66 pp |

Job role runs from Sales Representative at **39.76%** to Research Director at **2.50%**. Overtime is a strong and operationally actionable split, but it is not the widest split in this file.

## Two actionable signals

Employees working overtime leave at **30.53%** against **10.44%** for those who do not, a gap of **20.09 points**. Mean monthly income is **USD 4,787.09** for leavers and **USD 6,832.74** for stayers, so leavers earn **29.94% less on average**; that figure divides the difference by the stayers mean. The point-biserial correlation between income and attrition is **-0.16**, a weak ranking signal.

<Figure src="/images/data-analytics/hr-overtime.png" alt="Two bars comparing attrition for overtime and non-overtime employees, with overtime about 31 percent and non-overtime about 10 percent." caption="Overtime is a strong split, but job role spreads further." width="1280" height="544" />

Satisfaction is broadly decreasing, not strictly monotone: **22.84%** at level 1, **16.43%** at level 2, **16.52%** at level 3, and **11.33%** at level 4.

## Operational interpretation

Review workload first, then segment retention work by role before income. A fuller analysis would test whether changing workload changes the rate rather than assuming the association is causal.

## Where this stops

The data is synthetic, so nothing transfers to a real workforce. These are cross-sectional associations. The file does contain `PerformanceRating`, `YearsWithCurrManager`, and `YearsInCurrentRole`, but a single rating and tenure fields are not a longitudinal performance trajectory or a manager identifier, so neither performance over time nor a manager effect can be established from them. There are no confidence intervals and no correction across the many categorical comparisons, so the ranking is descriptive. The numbers demonstrate method and communication, not a claim about any company.
