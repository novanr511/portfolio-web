## The question

An HR analytics request usually arrives as "predict who will leave". The more useful analyst question is "which observable condition separates leavers from stayers, and is the gap large enough to act on". This demo answers that with a public employee attrition table and stops short of any causal claim.

## Dataset and source

The [IBM HR Attrition dataset](https://github.com/mrc03/IBM-HR-Analytics-Employee-Attrition-Performance/blob/master/WA_Fn-UseC_-HR-Employee-Attrition.csv) contains 1,470 employee records with attrition status, department, role, overtime, satisfaction, and income. The file is a synthetic teaching dataset created by IBM, not a record of real employees. Every figure below is computed by `scripts/build_data_analytics_demos.py` from the mirror linked here.

## Findings

Overall attrition is **16.12%**. I ranked every categorical split available in the file before naming one. Job role has the widest spread at **37.26 percentage points**, from Sales Representative at **39.76%** to Research Director at **2.50%**. Job involvement follows at **24.71 points**, job level at **21.62 points**, and overtime at **20.09 points**.

| Field | Spread across categories | Best single-category gap |
|---|---:|---:|
| JobRole | 37.26 pp | Sales Representative, 25.05 pp |
| JobInvolvement | 24.71 pp | level 1, 17.61 pp |
| JobLevel | 21.62 pp | level 4, 11.41 pp |
| OverTime | 20.09 pp | Yes, 14.41 pp |
| WorkLifeBalance | 17.03 pp | level 1, 15.13 pp |

The point of ranking them is to avoid naming a "strongest" split without having checked the alternatives. Overtime is a strong and operationally actionable split: **30.53%** for employees working overtime against **10.44%** for those who do not. It is not the widest split in this file.

<Figure src="/images/data-analytics/hr-overtime.png" alt="Two bars comparing attrition for overtime and non-overtime employees, with overtime about 31 percent and non-overtime about 10 percent." caption="Overtime is a strong split, but job role spreads further." width="1280" height="544" />

Income is a weak ranking signal here. Mean monthly income is **USD 4,787.09** for leavers and **USD 6,832.74** for stayers, so leavers earn **29.94% less on average**, computed as the difference divided by the stayers mean. The point-biserial correlation between monthly income and attrition is **-0.16**. Job satisfaction is broadly decreasing, though not strictly monotone: **22.84%** at level 1, **16.43%** at level 2, **16.52%** at level 3, and **11.33%** at level 4.

## What a people team would do with it

Treat overtime as an operational review trigger, not a prediction of individual departure. Segment retention work by role first, since the role spread is larger, then by overtime and satisfaction. A fuller analysis would test whether changing workload changes the rate.

## Limitations

The dataset is synthetic, so no finding transfers to a real workforce. These are cross-sectional associations, not causes. The file has no longitudinal performance trajectory and no manager identifier, so neither performance over time nor manager effect can be examined. There are no confidence intervals or correction across the many categorical comparisons, so the ranking is descriptive, not a set of confirmed effects. The numbers demonstrate method and communication, not a claim about any company.
