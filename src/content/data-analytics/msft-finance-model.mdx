## The question

A finance team does not ask for a share-price chart. It asks which operating lever moves profit, and how wrong the answer looks when conditions change. This page uses Microsoft's reported annual results to answer both, then marks every forward-looking number as an assumption. The Olist study was about a messy dataset; this one is about a budget line.

## Source and reported history

The source is the [SEC XBRL companyfacts API](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json) for Microsoft Corp, CIK 0000789019. XBRL is the tagging standard the SEC uses for filing data, a 10-K is the annual report, and CIK is the SEC's company identifier. `scripts/build_msft_finance_model.py` selects annual 10-K facts with a June fiscal year-end and keeps the most recently filed value for each year. Gross margin is gross profit divided by revenue; operating margin is operating income divided by revenue. Capex means spending on fixed assets. Every figure in the table is a reported fact in USD millions, not an estimate.

Revenue grew from USD 198,270 million in FY2022 to USD 331,839 million in FY2026. Gross margin stayed within a range of 67.94% to 69.76% while revenue nearly doubled. Operating margin rose from 42.06% to 46.78%. We project three years forward with three drivers: revenue growth, gross margin, and operating cost ratios.

## Model structure and assumptions

R&D and other operating costs are each held at its own five-year median share of revenue. Revenue growth and gross margin also come from five-year medians.

| Driver | Value | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Revenue growth | 15.30% | ILLUSTRATIVE, median of the four FY2023-FY2026 growth rates |
| Gross margin | 68.82% | ILLUSTRATIVE, median of reported margins |
| R&D as % of revenue | 12.04% | ILLUSTRATIVE, median of reported ratios |
| Other opex as % of revenue | 13.08% | ILLUSTRATIVE, derived from gross profit less R&D less operating income |

Every number in this table is a choice made for this exercise, not Microsoft guidance and not a prediction. The historical inputs are reported facts; the projections below are arithmetic on stated assumptions, so those numbers are illustrative as well.

Under the base assumptions, FY2029 revenue reaches USD 508,646 million and operating income USD 222,278 million, both ILLUSTRATIVE, for an operating margin of 43.70%. That margin sits 3.08 percentage points below the FY2026 reported margin of 46.78%, because the five-year median cost ratios are 25.12% of revenue against 21.17% in FY2026. The model is deliberately not fitted to the endpoint.

## Sensitivity: growth versus margin

The grid changes revenue growth within plus or minus three points and gross margin within plus or minus two points, holding other ratios at their medians. It answers a narrower question than a forecast: how far does the FY2029 operating-income line move inside this chosen range?

At the base growth of 15.30%, moving gross margin from 66.82% to 70.82% raises FY2029 operating income by USD 20,346 million across four points, about USD 5,087 million per point, all ILLUSTRATIVE. At the base margin of 68.82%, moving growth from 12.30% to 18.30% raises it by USD 34,709 million across six points, about USD 5,785 million per point, also ILLUSTRATIVE. Growth is about 14% stronger per point, and the growth range tested is wider, so its total swing is larger. The comparison depends on the range chosen, not on a probability.

## Decision memo

**Decision:** Start with growth by business segment. In this model, one extra point of revenue growth adds about USD 5,785 million (ILLUSTRATIVE) to FY2029 operating income, and the tested growth range is wider than the margin range. Gross-margin composition is the second question; one point adds about USD 5,087 million here (ILLUSTRATIVE).

**Owner:** finance planning lead, with an analyst maintaining the driver table. **Timeline:** one week to validate reported inputs against the 10-K, two weeks to replace the median ratios with segment-level drivers, one week to present a range instead of a point estimate.

**Not claimed:** no revenue, cost, or margin outcome was achieved, no owner was assigned, and no timeline was committed. The memo is a proposal built on public filings and illustrative assumptions.

## Limitations

This covers one company, five reported years, and three forward years. It uses consolidated figures because the SEC companyfacts endpoint does not carry segment dimensions; a real planning model would need the 10-K segment tables. No balance-sheet or cash-flow forecast is built here, so this is not a full three-statement model. Capex jumped from USD 44,477 million in FY2024 to USD 115,948 million in FY2026, which makes a constant-ratio cash-flow projection misleading. The scenarios are arithmetic, not probabilities, and no guidance from the company is used or implied.
