## The question

A retail manager does not need another chart of total revenue. The useful question is where margin leaks, and whether discounting is buying the volume it claims to buy. This demo treats a public retail transaction table as if it were a monthly management pack, then reports what the decision would be.

## Dataset and source

The [Sample Superstore dataset](https://github.com/yashbhanu/EDA--Exploratory-Data-Analysis--SampleSuperstore/blob/main/SampleSuperstore.csv) contains 9,994 order lines with sales, quantity, discount, profit, region, category, and segment. It is a public teaching dataset from a US retailer, not a private company file. Every figure below is computed by `scripts/build_data_analytics_demos.py` from that CSV.

## Findings

Total sales are **USD 2,297,200.86** and total profit is **USD 286,397.02**, a margin of **12.47%**. The Consumer segment contributes the most revenue at **USD 1,161,401.34**. Technology is the most profitable category, while Furniture contributes the least profit at **USD 18,451.27** despite being a major revenue line.

The discount question is where the useful signal sits. Across order lines, discount and profit margin have a correlation of **-0.86**. Grouped by discount band, aggregate margin falls from **29.51%** at no discount, to **12.75%** at 1% to 15%, **9.21%** at 16% to 30%, then turns negative at **-24.80%** for 31% to 50% and **-119.20%** above 50%. **18.72%** of order lines carry a negative profit.

## What a manager would do with it

Set a discount ceiling tied to category margin rather than a blanket percentage, and review Furniture separately because its revenue hides weak profit contribution. The next step in a real engagement would be an elasticity test, because this table cannot tell us how many discounted orders would have happened at full price.

## Limitations

This is a public teaching dataset with no customer identifiers, no costs beyond the supplied profit column, and no time dimension for seasonality. The margin by discount band is an aggregate ratio and is sensitive to a few large orders in the top bands. Nothing here establishes causation, and no result from this dataset is claimed as evidence about a real company.
