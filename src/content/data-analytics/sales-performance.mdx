## Source and shape

The 9,994 order lines from the [Sample Superstore mirror](https://github.com/yashbhanu/EDA--Exploratory-Data-Analysis--SampleSuperstore/blob/main/SampleSuperstore.csv) carry sales, quantity, discount, profit, region, category, and segment. This mirror is missing the `Order Date` and `Customer ID` columns of the canonical Superstore file. That absence shapes everything below: no seasonality, no repeat-customer view, no way to tell whether a discounted line would have happened at full price. The dataset is a public teaching set, not company books. All figures come from `scripts/build_data_analytics_demos.py`.

## Revenue and profit diverge

Every figure in this section is source-reported from the public CSV and computed by the script named above.

Total sales are **USD 2,297,200.86** against **USD 286,397.02** profit, a margin of **12.47%**. The Consumer segment brings in the most revenue at **USD 1,161,401.34**. Technology is the most profitable category. Furniture contributes the least profit at **USD 18,451.27**, despite being a major revenue line.

## Discount bands are where margin disappears

Grouping lines by discount gives aggregate margins of **29.51%** at no discount, **12.75%** at 1% to 15%, **9.21%** at 16% to 30%, then **-24.80%** at 31% to 50% and **-119.20%** above 50%. **18.72%** of all lines carry negative profit. The top band is not a thin tail of outliers: it holds **856 lines, all loss-making**, and its five largest losses make up **25.17%** of the band's loss.

Measured by eta-squared, discount band accounts for **0.7573** of variation in row margin, against **0.0084** for category and **0.0006** for segment. That is a comparison of grouping strength, not a causal estimate.

## Where I would investigate next

The first follow-up is an elasticity test, because this table cannot show how many discounted lines would have occurred at full price. A discount ceiling is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion this data supports.

## What the table cannot tell us

`Profit` is a supplied derived field and `Sales` is net of discount, so part of the **-0.86** correlation between discount and row margin is mechanical: lowering the denominator lowers the ratio by construction. The margin by band is an aggregate ratio and depends on how lines are grouped. There are no order identifiers, no time dimension, and no outcome after any decision. Nothing here establishes an effect, and nothing here is evidence about a real company.
