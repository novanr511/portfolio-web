## Business request

This brief is self-defined, not work done for a client. It asks one question a retail operations manager would actually ask: **which customer states and sellers carry the most revenue exposure to late delivery, and where should a review start?**

Three user stories were written before any query:

- As an operations lead, I want a late-delivery rate by state so I can see where the problem is concentrated.
- As a commercial lead, I want the BRL value of GMV exposed to late delivery so I can size the review.
- As a data analyst, I want the result reproducible from raw tables so a reviewer can challenge every number.

The accepted definition is stated up front: a delivery is late when `order_delivered_customer_date` is strictly later than `order_estimated_delivery_date`. Estimated dates are midnight in this dataset, so an order delivered on the promised calendar day after midnight counts as late. Under a date-only definition the same data gives 6,535 late orders instead of 7,827, which is why the definition matters more than the chart styling. GMV is the sum of `price` in `order_items`. GMV exposed to lateness is not lost revenue; it is the value of orders that arrived after the promise.

## Source and model

The [Olist Brazilian E-commerce public dataset](https://github.com/ruan-narici/brazilian-ecommerce-analytics/tree/main/src/data) contains 99,441 orders and 112,650 line items from September 2016 to October 2018. Eight raw CSVs were loaded into SQLite with the schema in [00_schema.sql](/sql/olist/00_schema.sql).

<Figure src="/images/olist/schema.png" alt="A fact table for orders at one row per order, joined to customer, date, seller and product dimensions with their keys." caption="Analytical model: one fact table at order grain, four dimensions, and order_items as the line-item bridge." width="1600" height="704" />

The grain matters. Order status lives at order grain; price and seller live at line-item grain. Any query that mixes them without an explicit join will double-count. The model and every query are in [sql/olist](/sql/olist), with the computed results in [olist-metrics.json](/sql/olist/olist-metrics.json).

## What the queries found

Of 99,441 orders, 96,478 reached delivered status. **7,827 deliveries were late, a rate of 8.11%** across all delivered orders. Among states with at least 100 delivered orders, Alagoas leads at **23.93%**, followed by Maranhão at **19.67%**, Piauí at **15.97%**, Ceará at **15.32%**, and Sergipe at **15.22%** ([03_late_rate_by_state.sql](/sql/olist/03_late_rate_by_state.sql)).

<Figure src="/images/olist/state-late-rate.png" alt="Bar chart of late-delivery rate by state, with Alagoas near 24 percent and Sao Paulo under 6 percent." caption="Late-delivery rate by state, states with at least 100 delivered orders." width="1280" height="544" />

The BRL exposure is concentrated. GMV on late-delivered items is **R$1,158,950.50**. São Paulo contributes **R$342,232.40**, Rio de Janeiro **R$238,881.29**, and the top five states (SP, RJ, MG, BA, RS) together hold **68.88%** of the exposed GMV ([04_late_gmv_by_state.sql](/sql/olist/04_late_gmv_by_state.sql)). Rate and value rank different states: Alagoas is the worst rate but only 14th by exposed GMV.

<Figure src="/images/olist/late-gmv-state.png" alt="Bar chart of GMV exposed to late delivery by state, with Sao Paulo and Rio de Janeiro far ahead." caption="GMV exposed to late delivery, top five states, BRL." width="1280" height="544" />

The seller view answers the second user story. Among sellers with at least 100 line items, the highest late rate is **24.53%** on 106 items, followed by **23.46%** on 405 items and **19.70%** on 132 items ([05_seller_delivery_risk.sql](/sql/olist/05_seller_delivery_risk.sql)). These are the operational units to audit; the state view alone cannot tell a manager which contract to review.

Cancellation is a separate, smaller problem: **625 orders** carry `canceled` status in the order table, of which **461** contain at least one line item, worth **R$95,235.27** ([06_cancellation_gmv.sql](/sql/olist/06_cancellation_gmv.sql)). Reviews confirm that lateness is visible to customers: mean review score is **2.567** on late deliveries against **4.214** for the band of on-time deliveries and orders with no recorded delivery date ([07_review_score_vs_late.sql](/sql/olist/07_review_score_vs_late.sql)). That second band is mixed, so the comparison is directional only.

<Figure src="/images/olist/monthly-gmv.png" alt="Line chart of monthly GMV over the first 24 months of the dataset." caption="Monthly GMV context. The late-delivery problem sits inside a growing order book." width="1280" height="512" />

## Decision memo

**Decision:** start a 30-day delivery review in the five states holding 68.88% of late-delivery GMV (SP, RJ, MG, BA, RS), then audit Alagoas and Maranhão separately as rate outliers that do not rank by exposed value.

**Owner:** logistics operations lead, with an analyst assigned to maintain the state-level query. **Timeline:** week 1 confirm the estimate dates are accurate, week 2 audit carrier handoffs in the top five states, week 3 report a revised late rate, week 4 decide whether to change carrier allocation.

**Evidence:** R$1,158,950.50 of GMV exposed, 7,827 late deliveries, and a review-score gap of 1.65 points. **Counterfactual not estimated:** this dataset contains no intervention, so no saving is claimed.

## Data quality checks

The queries in [11_quality_duplicates.sql](/sql/olist/11_quality_duplicates.sql) and [12_quality_referential_integrity.sql](/sql/olist/12_quality_referential_integrity.sql) found zero orphan foreign keys and zero duplicate order, customer, or item keys. They did find **789 reused `review_id` values**, producing **814 excess rows** in the reviews table. Each reused ID maps to a different order, so the review-score comparison describes review rows, not 99,224 unique reviews. That is a real limitation of the source, not a cleaning choice.

## Limitations

The data ends in October 2018, so this cannot describe current operations. It is observational: no carrier change, no experiment, and no before-and-after measurement. GMV exposed to lateness is not lost revenue, since those orders were delivered and presumably paid for. The brief is self-defined and no stakeholder signed off on the metric definitions, so the decision memo is a proposal, not a record of an implemented action. The 814 duplicate review IDs mean the review comparison is approximate.
