## The series

A return of roughly ten percent over two years looks unremarkable until the drawdown is placed beside it. This demo takes 506 trading days of [Apple OHLC from plotly](https://github.com/plotly/datasets/blob/master/finance-charts-apple.csv), adjusted for splits and dividends, running from 2015-02-17 to 2017-02-16. The window ends in February 2017 and is stale for any current decision. Every figure is computed by `scripts/build_data_analytics_demos.py` from unrounded prices; endpoints shown here are rounded.

## Return hides the path

Adjusted close moves from **122.91** to **135.35**. Compounding daily returns of the unrounded series gives **10.13%** over the window; the rounded endpoints alone give 10.12%. Returns are positive on **50.1%** of days. Annualized volatility, daily standard deviation scaled by the square root of 252, is **24.27%**. The maximum peak-to-trough decline is **-30.44%**.

The largest loss occurred well before the end of the window." width="1280" height="512" />

## Tail risk with its uncertainty

The fifth percentile of daily returns gives a 95% one-day historical VaR near **-2.5%**. A bootstrap of 2,000 resamples places that percentile between **-2.67% and -2.20%**, so quoting two decimals would overstate precision. VaR also omits the tail: expected shortfall at 95% is **-3.45%**, the worst observed day is **-6.57%**, and excess kurtosis is **3.11**.

## What this is not

One asset, one historical window, no forecast. Historical VaR assumes the next distribution resembles the observed one; here absolute returns have lag-1 autocorrelation of **0.14**, so the assumption is approximate. There is no benchmark, risk-free rate, transaction cost, or slippage. A complete assessment would add all of them and a more recent window before anyone acted on the figure. No economic claim is made, and nothing here is investment advice.
