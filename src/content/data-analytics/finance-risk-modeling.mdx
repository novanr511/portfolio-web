## The question

A risk summary is only as honest as its uncertainty. This demo takes a public daily price series and asks what belongs beside a return figure: volatility, drawdown, a tail-loss estimate, and the precision of that estimate. It makes no forecast and claims no edge.

## Dataset and source

The [Apple OHLC dataset from plotly](https://github.com/plotly/datasets/blob/master/finance-charts-apple.csv) contains 506 trading days from 2015-02-17 to 2017-02-16 with an adjusted close column sourced from Yahoo Finance. The window ends in February 2017 and is stale for any current risk decision. The adjusted series accounts for splits and dividends. Every figure below is computed by `scripts/build_data_analytics_demos.py`, which reads the unrounded prices; the endpoints shown here are rounded for display.

## Findings

Adjusted close moves from **122.91** to **135.35**. Compounding the daily returns of the unrounded series gives **10.13%** over the window; the rounded endpoints alone give 10.12%, so the quoted figure comes from the full precision data. Daily returns are positive on **50.1%** of days. Annualized volatility, the standard deviation of daily returns scaled by the square root of 252, is **24.27%**.

The drawdown view is the part a return headline omits. The maximum peak-to-trough decline is **-30.44%**. The fifth percentile of daily returns gives a 95% one-day historical VaR of about **-2.5%**. A bootstrap of 2,000 resamples puts the 95% interval for that percentile at **-2.67% to -2.20%**, so two decimal places would overstate precision.

VaR also hides the tail. Expected shortfall at 95% is **-3.45%**, the worst observed day is **-6.57%**, and excess kurtosis is **3.11**. A risk reader needs those numbers beside VaR, not in a footnote.

## What a finance team would do with it

Report return and tail risk together, with the uncertainty of the estimate. A 10% two-year return with a 30% drawdown is a different proposition from the same return with a 5% drawdown. A complete assessment would add a benchmark, a cost model, a position-sizing rule, and a more recent window before anyone acted on the figure.

## Limitations

This is one asset over one historical window ending February 2017, not a diversified portfolio and not a forecast. Historical VaR assumes the next return distribution resembles the observed one. That assumption is approximate here: absolute returns have lag-1 autocorrelation of **0.14**, and there is no benchmark, risk-free rate, transaction cost, or slippage in the window. No economic claim is made, and nothing here is investment advice.
