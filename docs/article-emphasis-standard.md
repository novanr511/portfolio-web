# Article emphasis standard

This document defines the reusable emphasis vocabulary for article MDX files. It
exists so every article gets the same *system* of emphasis without getting the
same *sentences*. Standardize the components, not the copy: a reader who opens
two unrelated articles should see the same hierarchy signals, but each signal
must carry that article's own sourced content.

## Dial (R-37)

Reading this as: a calm editorial article for a general reader, in the existing
restrained typographic language, dial **ENERGY 1 / RHYTHM 1 / MOTION 1**.
Liveliness comes from contrast in scale and weight, never from decoration.

## Vocabulary

| Component | Role | When to use | Reason (R-31) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `<Lead>` | Larger opening paragraph | Once, on the first paragraph after frontmatter | Marks the entry point without motion |
| `<KeyPoint>` | Claim with a left accent rule | Once, on the single sentence the article exists to deliver | Gives a scanning reader the hierarchy cue |
| `<Mark>` | Faint highlight on a phrase | 1 to 4 times, on phrases already present in the text | Marks importance without underlining, which links use |
| `<KeyFigures>` | Sourced numbers as rows | Only when the article already contains a number with a named source | Keeps evidence attached to the value (R-17) |
| `<Trap>` | Left-rule aside labelled "The trap" | Once, on a common mistake already described in the article | Flags a pitfall without a pill badge (R-09) |
| `<PullQuote>` | Oversized italic line | Once, on the sharpest existing sentence | One focal point per screen |

## Hard rules

1. **No new claims.** Every word inside an emphasis component must already
   exist in the article or be a direct restatement of its sourced content.
2. **No sourced-number fabrication.** `<KeyFigures>` is omitted entirely when
   the article has no number tied to a named source. An empty slot is better
   than an invented statistic (R-17, R-38).
3. **Illustrative numbers are labelled `Illustrative`** in the same row.
4. **One of each component per article**, except `<Mark>` which is capped at
   four. Repetition turns hierarchy into decoration.
5. **No em dash in agent-written text.** Use comma, period, colon, or
   parentheses (R-02). Em dashes already present in the References list are
   generated by the registry script and are left alone.
6. **Sources stay in the registry.** A `<KeyFigures>` row names a source that
   is already in `docs/further-reading-registry.json` for that article.
7. **Mobile safe.** Every component must render without horizontal overflow
   at 390px, with WCAG AA contrast in both themes (R-03, R-25).

## Placement order

A standard article reads in this order:

1. `<Lead>` opening paragraph
2. `**Direct answer:**` and `**Delta:**` (existing)
3. `## The mechanism` with `<KeyPoint>` and `<Mark>`
4. `<Figure>` where a diagram already exists
5. `<KeyFigures>` only if sourced numbers exist
6. `<Trap>` on the named mistake
7. `<PullQuote>` on the sharpest line, before `## What it cannot do`
8. `## Evidence status`, `## What it cannot do`, `## A decision test`
9. `## References & Further Reading`

## Rollout

Apply in batches with disjoint article sets. After each batch:

- `node scripts/apply-further-reading.mjs`
- `node scripts/check-mdx.mjs`
- `node scripts/check-articles.mjs --report`
- `npx tsc --noEmit`
- `npm run lint`
- `git diff --check`

Then commit with a targeted `git add`, never `git add -A`.
